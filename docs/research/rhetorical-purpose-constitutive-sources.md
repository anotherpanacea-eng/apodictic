## Source account

Charland contrasts audience constitution with an assumed pre-existing audience (134). His Quebec independence analysis presents collective and transhistorical subjects, whose narrative constrains apparent freedom of action (139–141). Interpellation can fail; embodied action matters (141). He acknowledges a circumvented ontology question and mutable positions (147). The account is ideological and historical (148).

## Editorial inference

For the [profile](../rhetorical-purpose-pluralism-spec.md#1-at-table-dialectical-claritymd-step-1), map enacted membership against undertaken promises. Constituting a bounded collective does not automatically violate universal inclusion. Textual mechanisms cannot certify reception; retained factual and consequential commitments still require applicable checks.
