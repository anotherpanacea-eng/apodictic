## Source A: Silence and Institutional Prejudice

[Author-hosted manuscript](https://www.mirandafricker.com/uploads/1/3/6/2/136236203/silence_and_institutional_prejudice.pdf), printed pages 4-5, 10-15 (PDF indices 3-4, 9-14).

**Source account.** Innocent ignorance of expertise differs from prejudice-driven credibility deflation (pp. 4-5). Correction restores appropriate credibility; unclear credibility permits further evidence or reserved judgment, not automatic belief (p. 10). Institutional group attitudes can differ from members' private attitudes (pp. 11-15).

**Own editorial inference.** Fair consideration is not compelled belief. Inspect receiving practices without reducing institutional conduct to private intentions or form completeness.

## Source B: Evolving Concepts of Epistemic Injustice

[Author-hosted manuscript](https://www.mirandafricker.com/uploads/1/3/6/2/136236203/evolving_concepts_of_epistemic_injustice.pdf), printed pages 1-4, 8-10, 12 (PDF indices 0-3, 7-9, 11).

**Source account.** Discriminatory and distributive epistemic injustice are distinct, overlapping categories. Prejudice-clouded credibility misjudgment differs from deliberate manipulation; hermeneutical marginalization concerns unequal interpretive opportunity (pp. 1-4). Marginalized lives offer a methodological starting point; structural preemptive exclusion can prevent witnesses being sought (pp. 8-10).

**Own editorial inference.** Marginalized perspective is a methodological lead, not an automatic guarantee of knowledge (p. 12). Distinguish being discounted from never being invited; missing information alone does not establish prejudice.
