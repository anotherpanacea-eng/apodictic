# Source 2: Alternative approaches to argument

- **Model:** Claude Opus 5.5 with web research
- **Date:** 2026-10-02
- **Method:** five parallel research passes plus synthesis
- **Shape:** 19 approaches (one of them a design bridge); no class D; one new AT row (AT6, constitutive/relational address), an AT4 split extension, and ten finding rules, with C0 kept mandatory and typed as assertion or address.

## Headline

Most of what the engine misreads in constitutive, narrative, witness, jeremiadic, and invitational texts is a span-applicability problem: a question that fits an asserting span (where is the warrant, where is the strongest objection) is being asked of a span that greets, hails, witnesses, or commemorates. The leanest fix is one new argument type, **AT6 Constitutive / relational address**, modeled on AT5's split burden (coherence of the address across the piece, asserted burden at each cash-out), plus an **AT4 extension** (representative burden may rest on a named mandate; a new attribution sub-burden for mediated or recorded speech), plus a short list of **applicability rules** that switch specific questions off for confirmed span functions. Every factual, causal, quantified, historical, attributive, and prescriptive claim keeps its burden inside every approach, and stance triage cannot earn any of them. This source finds **no approach that needs class D**. On the C0 question it parts company with Source 1's headline: a solidarity address does have a statable point, so the engine should keep C0 mandatory and let it be typed as an **address C0** (what the text asks the reader to be, recognize, or do), with the document-level warrant verdict recorded as not applicable and verdicts moved to cash-out spans. Removing C0 would also remove CL0's ability to tell a solidarity address from a text that never commits to anything. The empirical record is mixed in a way that matters for the critique: narrative effects are real but small and the meta-analyses conflict on print, while O'Keefe's meta-analyses show that explicit conclusions and refutation of objections are not persuasively costly, so the engine's existing norms for asserting spans should stay.

## 1. Constitutive rhetoric (Charland, Black, McGee; second and third persona)

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
Constitutive rhetoric forms the collective subject that later persuasion addresses. Charland, drawing on Althusser's interpellation, names three effects: a collective subject that transcends individual divisions, a transhistorical subject linking past and present members toward a telos, and an "illusion of freedom" in which subjects must act consistently with a narrative already written; the narrative is left incomplete so that closure falls to the audience's action. McGee treats "the people" as a mythic construct that exists only when individuals accept living inside it. Black's second persona is the implied auditor the text asks the real audience to become, and Wander's third persona is the audience the text negates through what it says and leaves unsaid. "Effective" means a formed identity whose members act. The evidence is interpretive rhetorical criticism of historical cases (Quebec sovereignty, nationalist, feminist, and civil-rights discourse); **no experiment was found testing interpellative address against claim-based messaging**. The nearest quantitative finding is correlational: in campaign launch speeches across 43 Australian federal elections, winners used "we/us" about once every 79 words against once every 136 for losers and out-used opponents in 80% of elections, and the authors say the archival design cannot show causal direction (Steffens & Haslam 2013). Evidence: strong as interpretation, thin as causal evidence.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
A named collective; a pronoun pattern (I / we / you / they) in which "we" or "you" carries the work; shared-past narration that runs into an open present; a role or act handed to the reader at the close; an implied auditor whose values a reader can reconstruct from the text's assumptions; people discussed only in the third person (the third-persona trace).

### 3. Success criteria
The "we" is identifiable and either stable or visibly widened; the implied auditor is one the stated audience can occupy, with a path into the role (an act, or a value already shared); the narrative closes in a role or act; exclusions read as choices the text owns.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Uninhabitable implied auditor** (a letter to legislators that hails them as fellow survivors). Guard: aspirational interpellation is legitimate when the text supplies a path into the role; flag only when no path exists or when stated audience and implied auditor contradict each other.
- **Silent excluding "we."** Guard: a bounded "we" whose boundary is named ("we who work in this system") is a choice; flag only unmarked scope.
- **"We" drift** (movement, then nation, then humanity, with no transition). Guard: a deliberate widening is a classic constitutive climax; flag only when the widening is unmarked and a claim depends on the narrower scope.
- **Narrative with no closure-by-action.** Guard: commemorative and epideictic texts may intend reflection; ask the writer.
- **Contestable fact inside the shared history.** Guard: commonly accepted history (a founding document, a dated event) needs no citation; flag a contestable causal or quantified insertion.

### 5. Editorial advice class
Pronoun-scope questions ("Is the 'we' in paragraph 3 the same 'we' as in paragraph 9?"); path-into-role questions ("What would a skeptical member of your stated audience need to recognize in themselves to accept this 'we'?"); asking whether a widening should be noticed; separating shared values from contestable facts; naming the act the text hands the reader. The editor asks; the writer supplies the collective, the role, and the wording.

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
Step 2 forces a propositional C0 ("Quebec should be sovereign") when the text's work is "you are this people, and this people acts thus"; the identity claim is then scored as an unsupported subclaim. The "we" is read as an unwarranted generalization, shared-history narration as uncited assertion, and the absence of opponent engagement as an OB failure. Meanwhile the real failures (pronoun drift, an uninhabitable auditor, third-persona exclusion, facts smuggled in as shared history) produce no finding, because none of them is a missing warrant.

### 7. Classification
**B.** Its success criteria (inhabitable auditor, consistent collective, closure-by-action) are burdens no AT1–AT5 row states, so it needs a new row (AT6) with an AT5-style split; factual and causal cash-outs keep full burden.

## 2. Burkean identification

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
Burke's identification seeks consubstantiality, a shared substance of occupations, beliefs, values, and objects, motivated by the division between persons; it supplements traditional persuasion and can replace it. His formula holds that "you persuade a man only insofar as you can talk his language." Burke distinguishes identification by sympathy (common ground), by antithesis (uniting against a common opponent), and by *false assumption* (identification the audience does not consciously register). Cheney carried the concept into organizational communication, where identification compensates for estrangement in the division of labor, and follow-on organizational work describes it as a form of unobtrusive control. "Effective" means felt shared substance that makes the speaker's ends the audience's ends. Evidence: theoretical and critical, with partial correlational support from pronoun studies (Steffens & Haslam 2013, above). Burke's own analysis of *Mein Kampf* shows the same mechanisms (the unifying voice, the scapegoat) serving harm. Moderate to thin.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
Shared vocabulary and biographical or occupational markers ("as a nurse," "as any parent knows"); explicitly shared values; a named common opponent; inclusive framing the reader may not register as a move; claims introduced as what "we all know."

### 3. Success criteria
The shared substance named is one the text's stated audience holds as the text itself frames that audience; identification prepares claims that carry their own support; antithesis is bounded and the "we" has positive content of its own.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Smuggled claim**: a factual, causal, or quantified claim rides in as shared knowledge ("as we all know, X causes Y"). Guard: shared values and widely held premises need no proof; flag only premises that are empirical and contestable for the stated audience.
- **Antithesis overreach**: unity built mainly by naming an enemy, with the out-group characterized but never addressed. Guard: naming an opponent is often necessary (protest, jeremiad); flag when the antithesis carries an unsupported factual charge.
- **Commonality that erases difference or power** (Ratcliffe; Lyon's "magical thinking"). Guard: raise as a question about the limits of the "we"; it is never a defect on its own.

### 5. Editorial advice class
"Is X a value your audience holds or an empirical claim they could dispute?"; "The opponent is vivid; what is the positive content of the collective?"; "Do you want to acknowledge where the 'we' stops?"

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
Common-ground passages read as padding or as appeal to popularity. The engine's existing DI0 (starting-point smuggling) and AC3 (sympathetic-audience flattery drift) partly catch the real failure, which is useful, but AC3 can also fire on identification that is doing legitimate preparatory work.

### 7. Classification
**C.** Identification usually serves an AT2, AT3, or AT6 text and needs only a shared-premise applicability rule (shared values produce no evidence finding) paired with the existing DI0 check for empirical premises framed as shared.

## 3. Narrative argument (Fisher's narrative paradigm; transportation and meta-analytic evidence)

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
Fisher's *homo narrans* argues by a coherent account of agents, motives, and consequences whose values the audience tests against its own experience. He offers two tests: probability or coherence (structural, material, characterological) and fidelity, operationalized as a "logic of good reasons" (the values in the message, their relevance, consequences, consistency with experienced values, and whether they are an ideal basis for conduct). Critics hold that narrative is one mode among others (Rowland 1987, 1989) and that a story can ring true for its audience and still be a bad story (Warnick 1987). The empirical literature tests a different construct, transportation (absorption in imagery, affect, attention): Green & Brock (2000, four experiments) found that transportation increased story-consistent beliefs, that highly transported readers found fewer "false notes," and that labeling the story fact or fiction had little effect. Meta-analytic effects are small and the meta-analyses do not agree:

| Meta-analysis | Scope | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Braddock & Dillard 2016 | narrative vs. none | beliefs k=37, N=7,376, r=.17; attitudes k=40, N=7,132, r=.19; intentions k=28, N=5,211, r=.17; behaviors k=5, N=978, r=.23; medium did not moderate |
| Shen, Sheer & Li 2015 | health narratives, 25 studies, N=9,330 | r=.063 overall; audio/video significant, **print not significant** |
| van Laer et al. 2014 | consumer transportation, 132 effect sizes from 76 articles | pooled effects not retrieved |
| Allen & Preiss 1997 | narrative vs. statistical, k=16, N=1,836 | r=.074 favoring statistical evidence |
| Zebregs et al. 2015 | narrative vs. statistical | statistics stronger on beliefs and attitude; narrative stronger on intention (sizes not retrieved) |

The print-narrative conflict (Braddock & Dillard find no medium moderator; Shen et al. find print null) bears directly on op-eds and written testimony and should be reported unresolved. "Effective" therefore means two different things here: Fisher's adherence grounded in recognized good reasons, and the meta-analyses' small short-term shifts measured mostly in health and consumer experiments. No study found operationalizes Fisher's coherence and fidelity as predictors, and transfer to policy writing is extrapolation. Behavioral evidence is thin (k=5).

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
An identifiable protagonist (name, age, place); concrete sequential particulars; a point of view; an uninterrupted narrative stretch; a cash-out sentence where the story becomes a claim ("this is why," "stories like hers"); values the story dramatizes; the placement of any statistics relative to the story.

### 3. Success criteria
The story is internally coherent; the cash-out claims the lesson the story actually shows; the values invoked at the cash-out are the values the story enacted; the cash-out asks no more than one case can bear; the text says whether the case is typical, illustrative, or exceptional.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Lesson drift**: the story shows X, the cash-out asserts Y. Guard: no finding when the writer bridges the gap with a stated warrant or a second source.
- **Exemplar as prevalence**: exemplification research (Zillmann & Brosius 2000) shows readers infer prevalence from vivid cases. Guard: no finding when the piece makes no frequency claim or marks the case as atypical or as an instance of a separately established pattern.
- **Coherence by omission** (Warnick's worry). Guard: the engine cannot see what was left out; flag only omissions visible on the page (a counter-fact stated elsewhere in the piece, a conspicuous sequence gap).
- **Fidelity read as truth.** Guard: do not downgrade a story for lacking external corroboration of its particulars unless those particulars are premises for a quantified, causal, or prescriptive claim.
- **Absorption with no cash-out** in a genre that requires an ask (testimony, regulatory comment). Guard: recognition-ending pieces (AT6, AT1) may legitimately end without an ask.

### 5. Editorial advice class
Locate and sharpen the cash-out; check the cash-out against what the story showed; name the values at the cash-out; state typicality; protect the narrative stretch from interruption and place prevalence data where it frames the story. The editor does not recommend "add statistics" by reflex (see Combining modes).

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
It treats a story as evidence without a warrant, fires hasty-generalization or NE1 on every vignette, and recommends more data, which may trade away the absorption mechanism for little belief gain. It also risks the inverse error: once a span is labeled narrative, letting the cash-out pass. And it can count a story's felt force as support for factual claims inside the story, which Green & Brock's fact/fiction result undercuts. The existing NE0–NE3 codes are well aimed; the problem is where they fire.

### 7. Classification
**C.** Narrative spans are observational or illustrative, and warrant and scope checks apply at the cash-out; the existing NE codes and AT5's cash-out logic already supply the machinery.

## 4. Witness and testimony (Fricker, Dotson, testimonio)

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
Witness persuades by presence and standing. Fricker's testimonial injustice is a credibility deficit owing to identity prejudice, which wrongs a speaker in her capacity as a knower. Dotson adds testimonial quieting (an audience fails to identify a speaker as a knower) and smothering (a speaker truncates testimony because the audience appears unable to give it uptake). Felman & Laub stress the listener's role in enabling testimony. Testimonio (Beverley 1989) is a first-person account whose "I" stands for a collective; the Menchú controversy (Stoll 1999) shows a text that can survive as a collective account while its personal particulars are disputed (the manner of her brother's death, her schooling, the land dispute). "Effective" splits three ways that the literatures do not reconcile: belief in reported events, recognition of the speaker as a knower, and representation of a collective. Evidence is philosophical and literary-critical. The closest empirical analog is Kubin et al. (2021, 15 studies): opponents respected moral positions more, and judged holders more rational, when positions rested on personal (especially harm-related) experience; the outcome measured was respect, and attitude change was not measured. Thin for written witness.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
First person; access markers ("I saw," "I was told," "in my twelve years at"); hedges marking the edge of what was seen; shifts from "I" to "we" or "people like me"; shifts from report to interpretation to prescription; provenance markers for recorded or translated speech (who recorded it, when, through whom).

### 3. Success criteria
Statements are marked as observation, hearsay, interpretation, or speaking-for; any representative claim states its basis (sample, role, or a named mandate such as an organization's testimony); interpretive and prescriptive claims carry their own support; composites and pseudonyms are disclosed; quoted speech names its witness text.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Unmarked representative slide**: "I" becomes "all of us," then a prescription. Guard: a signaled collective voice with a stated mandate is legitimate.
- **Undisclosed composite** presented as one real person. Guard: a disclosed composite converts the claim into a representative one, which AT4 already handles.
- **Corroboration demand as testimonial injustice**: the engine asks a witness for more corroboration than it would ask of an official. Guard: hold observational reports to one standard regardless of the speaker's position; reserve corroboration requests for interpretive, causal, quantified, and representative claims, which carry burden for any speaker. (This is a design inference from Fricker.)
- **Smothering-shaped gaps.** Guard: ask before flagging an "incomplete account."
- **Provenance collapse**: a mediated text graded as the named speaker's own testimony (Truth, Joseph). Guard: when the writer uses the text as a reception artifact, the attribution burden drops.

### 5. Editorial advice class
Mark the access basis of each observation; make the I-to-we shift explicit and state its warrant; separate what was seen from what it means; disclose composites; name the transmission chain for quoted speech; let the interpretive cash-out carry its own evidence.

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
AT4's split is well designed, but two misreadings remain: corroboration demands on observational spans (enacting the injustice the engine's own grounding cites) and credibility excess, where "I was there" is allowed to warrant interpretive or prescriptive claims. It also produces a "missing C0" reading for a text whose point is to be heard, and it has no check for mediated attribution.

### 7. Classification
**B.** A burden-split extension inside AT4: the representative burden accepts a named mandate as a basis, and a fourth sub-burden (attribution) covers recorded, translated, or reconstructed speech; the parity rule for observational spans is a C rule.

## 5. Invitational rhetoric (Foss & Griffin)

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
Foss & Griffin (1995) propose a non-persuasive address grounded in equality, immanent value, and self-determination. It works through offering perspectives (stating one's understanding without advocating it) and re-sourcement (declining a hostile frame by drawing on a different source), under three conditions the rhetor creates: safety, value, and freedom (no penalty if the audience does not agree). "Effective" means mutual understanding, so persuasion-effect evidence fits it poorly. Bone, Griffin & Scholz (2008) concede that applied research has been low. Critics argue that democracy depends on legitimate persuasion (Condit 1997) and that the theory presupposes an equality the oppressed lack (Lozano-Reich & Cloud 2009). The empirical work usually cited for it tests something else: deep canvassing (Broockman & Kalla 2016, 501 voters; Kalla & Broockman 2020, 6,869 voters, where arguments alone produced no detectable change and adding non-judgmental narrative exchange produced durable effects of d ≈ 0.08, replications d ≈ 0.08 and 0.04; Kalla & Broockman 2023 on perspective-getting) is interactive, elicits the listener's experience, and has a persuasive goal that Foss & Griffin disclaim. It supports narrative exchange in conversation more than invitational writing. Kubin et al. (2021) is the nearest analog to invitational success (respect from opponents). **Evidence for written, one-way invitational texts: thin and indirect; demoted in the value ranking on that basis.**

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
A perspective stated as one's own ("here is how I came to see this"); explicit freedom clauses; respectful representation of the reader's likely reasons; re-sourcement (stepping to a story, practice, or question instead of rebutting inside an opponent's frame); open questions; an absent or deliberately subordinated prescriptive claim.

### 3. Success criteria
Purpose coherence: the stated freedom is not contradicted by a covert ask. The offered perspective is concrete and owned. The other position is represented in terms its holders would recognize.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Covert persuasion**: a freedom clause followed by a prescriptive close or loaded framing. Guard: an invitational body with a separately marked ask is legitimate; flag only a disguised ask or one that contradicts the stated freedom.
- **Vacuity**: the reader cannot tell what the writer thinks. Guard: exploratory pieces may intend this; ask.
- **False equality** (Lozano-Reich & Cloud). Guard: a strategic choice the writer owns; raise only as a question, never as a defect or a political verdict.
- **Condescending safety moves**. Guard: warmth is not condescension; flag only presumptive claims about the reader's inner state.

### 5. Editorial advice class
Align declared purpose with actual asks (own the ask and mark the shift, or remove it); make the perspective more concrete instead of adding argument; check representation of the other side for recognizability; ask whether the power relation makes invitation the right choice.

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
It looks for C0 and finds a weak one, then fires CL0, OB0, and unsupported-claim findings, each a category error; it reads freedom clauses as hedging. It misses the real failure (a covert ask contradicting the stated freedom), which is a purpose-coherence problem. The existing GN0 (register instability) and GN2 (concealed cash-out) codes are the right shape for that failure.

### 7. Classification
**C.** With writer confirmation, objection-handling and asserted-C0 questions do not apply to invitational spans, and the covert-ask failure routes through GN2-style concealed cash-out logic; factual and prescriptive claims keep full burden.

## 6. Agonism (Mouffe; rhetorical uptake by Ivie and Engels)

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
Mouffe's agonistic pluralism treats conflict between legitimate adversaries as the substance of democratic politics and rejects rational consensus (Habermas, Rawls) as its aim; adversaries are opponents whose right to fight is respected, while enemies are to be destroyed. Connolly and Honig supply related accounts (respectful contestation; the excluded remainders of every settlement). Ivie analyzes war rhetoric's demonization of enemies, and Engels's *enemyship* names uniting citizens against shared enemies to stabilize an order. "Effective" means mobilizing one's side, contesting hegemonic common sense, and keeping conflict legitimate. Evidence is interpretive. The empirical work bearing on it is a counterweight: in 563,312 posts, each additional moral-emotional word raised diffusion by about 20%, more within ideological networks than between them (Brady et al. 2017), and affective polarization carries broad social costs (Iyengar et al. 2019). An editor can state the tradeoff (in-group energy at the cost of cross-group reach) without a verdict. Standard critiques: agonism covertly relies on shared rules and offers no mechanism for turning enemies into adversaries.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
A specific named adversary; the hegemonic frame being contested ("what everyone assumes is..."); an explicit refusal of consensus as the goal; acknowledgment of the adversary's standing to contest; a defined "us" with an invitation to join; the presence or absence of dehumanizing, eliminationist, or essentializing language, especially in metaphor.

### 3. Success criteria
The adversary is identifiable and characterized so they would recognize their own position; the contested frame is nameable; the adversary/enemy line holds throughout, including in metaphor; claims about the adversary's conduct meet ordinary burden.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Enemy slide**: denial of standing, dehumanization, essential evil. Guard: anger, heat, and incivility are not enemy construction; flag only denial of standing or dehumanization.
- **Phantom adversary**: a straw "they." Guard: manifestos may name systems; ask whether the system is specified.
- **Unearned attributions** about the adversary's conduct or motives (never exempt).
- **Mobilization without a frame**. Guard: a rally speech may rely on a frame its audience already holds.

### 5. Editorial advice class
Specify the adversary and their actual position; state the contested frame; audit labels and metaphors against the adversary/enemy line; separate the stance (exempt from deliberative steelmanning) from factual claims about the adversary (not exempt); if the writer also wants cross-group persuasion, name the tradeoff as a question.

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
It fires "fails to engage strongest opposing view" and "one-sided" and pushes toward consensus, which is the critique's "Toulmin-plus-steelmanning" point in practice. It can also over-credit a polished, hedged piece that quietly constructs an enemy, because enemy construction is a framing problem with no warrant signature.

### 7. Classification
**C.** With writer confirmation, the rhetorical-fairness question is replaced by an adversary-recognizability check and consensus stops being a criterion; the adversary/enemy check runs always and lives in Guardrails, outside any exemption.

## 7. Young's greeting, rhetoric, and narrative

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
In *Inclusion and Democracy* (2000; the chapter "Inclusive Political Communication," numbered 2 or 3 depending on the edition's count), Young adds greeting, rhetoric, and narrative to argument as legitimate communicative forms. Greeting establishes subject-to-subject recognition, answering a situation in which some groups are discussed in the third person instead of addressed. Rhetoric and narrative carry socially situated knowledge. "Internal exclusion" occurs when people are formally present but their contributions are discounted for their mode of expression (accent, grammar, emotional register). "Effective" means recognition and inclusion, the conditions under which a public will listen. Evidence: normative political theory only. The notes found no critique of greeting as ritual without substance, and the main review consulted (Jaeger) is sympathetic. Thin as evidence of effect; strong as a design caution.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
Direct second-person address to the people under discussion; acknowledgment of the addressee's presence, standing, or loss before the business; narrative conveying situated experience; register choices (emotional, vernacular) that a narrow rubric would read as defects.

### 3. Success criteria
Affected parties are addressed or the third person is a visible choice; the greeting connects to the business that follows; narrative spans meet the narrative criteria above.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Third-person-only treatment** of the people a text is about. Guard: third person can be deliberate (testimony to a body about absent parties); ask.
- **Greeting disconnected from business**. Guard: in ceremonial or condolence openings the relation is the business; ask the writer which.
- **Register penalized as a defect** (the engine's own failure). Guard: emotional or vernacular register is never by itself a finding.

### 5. Editorial advice class
"The people affected appear only in the third person; is there a place to address them directly, or is that deliberate?"; "How does the opening relationship carry into the ask?"

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
A greeting reads as throat-clearing or buried lede; register reads as imprecision; narrative reads as anecdote. Young's internal exclusion is close to an exact description of a rubric that only reports what a narrative failed to prove.

### 7. Classification
**C.** One applicability rule (register and greeting spans produce no finding) and one optional check (are affected parties addressed or only discussed); narrative portions are handled by AT4 and the narrative rule.

## 8. The American jeremiad

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
The jeremiad narrates a covenant or promise, a declension from it, and a restoration conditional on return. Bercovitch's American variant ends in optimism and channels dissent into renewed consensus (a "ritual of consensus"). Murphy (2009) separates a traditionalist jeremiad (return to the literal past) from a progressive one (renewal of past ideals, as King invoking the Declaration). Howard-Pitney traces the African American jeremiad from Douglass, Wells, and Du Bois through King, holding the nation to its stated ideals. "Effective" means mobilization and reaffirmed identity, with reform reframed as fidelity. Evidence: criticism and historical cases only; no experiments found. J. M. Murphy's study of Robert Kennedy's 1968 response to King's assassination gives the dual verdict: the form restores cohesion in crisis and forecloses close examination of the system that produced the crisis. Bentley adds that the form can calcify into resentment when real decline undercuts its optimism.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
An invoked founding text or promise; "we were meant to be"; a catalog of present betrayals; a conditional future ("if we return..., then..."); communal second-person-plural address; blame assigned to lapse instead of design.

### 3. Success criteria
The covenant is specified (which text, which principle); each declension item is concrete and, where factual, supported; the restoration follows from the invoked promise; the addressed "we" is identifiable.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Exclusionary covenant**: the promise historically excluded the people now invoked. Guard: no finding when the text thematizes the exclusion (Douglass turns it into the indictment).
- **Unearned declension**: "crime has never been worse" keeps ordinary burden.
- **Nostalgic falsification**: a golden age stated as historical fact keeps its burden.
- **Restoration with no route back**. Guard: a solidarity or witness address with no prescription is not deficient for lacking policy.
- Murphy's structural limit is **never** a finding (it would be a political verdict); at most a question if the writer's stated goal is systemic critique.

### 5. Editorial advice class
Name the covenant precisely; ask whether each declension item needs a source; check that the restoration follows from the promise; if the audience includes people the covenant excluded, ask whether the text addresses that.

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
It extracts the prophecy as C0 and demands causal evidence, and it reads the covenant as appeal to tradition. The correct reconstruction: the covenant is an audience-shared warrant, the declension is an evidence span at ordinary burden, and the restoration is either an AT3 prescription (if it names action) or an exhortation under the address C0.

### 7. Classification
**A.** It decomposes onto existing machinery plus the shared-premise rule (covenant), ordinary evidence burden (declension), and AT3 or AT6 cash-out handling (restoration); a genre hint for recognition is enough.

## 9. Prophetic and apocalyptic rhetoric (omission flagged)

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
Darsey (1997) traces American radical reform rhetoric (abolitionism, McCarthyism, gay-rights activism) to the Hebrew prophets: the speaker claims a mandate beyond the audience's consent, refuses compromise, and speaks in crisis time. Brummett (1991) and O'Leary (1994/1998) treat apocalyptic rhetoric; their specific frameworks (Brummett's tragic and comic frames, O'Leary's topoi) were not verified this session. It differs from the jeremiad in refusing the jeremiad's conditional optimism. "Effective" means moral clarification and mobilization of the committed. Evidence: criticism and historical cases; thin.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
Absolute moral language; refusal of trade-offs; the speaker positioned as messenger; crisis-time framing; predictions of reckoning.

### 3. Success criteria
Moral absolutes are legible as stance; predictions are marked as conditional or figurative, or else supported; the mandate claimed is stated.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Prediction stated as fact** (a dated or quantified outcome). Never exempt.
- **Unconditional end-time claim** load-bearing for a prescription. Guard: a figurative or conventional prophetic register with no prescriptive load can be earned by stance triage.

### 5. Editorial advice class
Separate the moral stance (which may stand without steelmanning) from forecasts (which carry evidence burden); mark which predictions are figures.

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
It flags refusal to steelman as rhetorical unfairness and overstatement as BP findings, while it may miss that a figurative prophecy has slid into a literal forecast.

### 7. Classification
**A.** Stance triage already earns moral absolutism as provocation and already blocks earning for prescriptions; add only that stance triage never earns a factual or quantified prediction (rule 5 below).

## 10. Perelman & Olbrechts-Tyteca: presence, values and hierarchies, loci

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
*The New Rhetoric* works on the starting points of argument. Presence (§29, "Selection of Data and Presence") makes selected elements vivid so they weigh more; values and hierarchies (§§18–20) argue by reordering what an audience already holds; loci (§§22–24: quantity, quality, order, the existent, essence, the person) are the general premises that make an ordering seem obvious. "Effective" means a shifted hierarchy or a reframed sense of what is relevant. Evidence: theoretical and critical; **no persuasion experiments on presence or loci as such were found** (vividness research is the likely analog and was not searched). Thin for effect sizes.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
Presence: disproportionate space, concrete detail, repetition, present tense, and conspicuous omission of other facts. Hierarchies: ranking language ("above all," "more fundamental than"), sacrifice and trade-off framing. Loci: "millions," "most" (quantity); "irreplaceable," "once lost" (quality); "root cause," "first" (order); "already happening" (existent); "what X really is" (essence); dignity over efficiency (person).

### 3. Success criteria
The most present material is the material the conclusion rests on; the hierarchy is stated or clearly inferable; loci are used consistently across the piece.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Presence mismatch**: vividness spent on a side point. Guard: a narrative opening that establishes stakes is fine if the stakes are cashed out.
- **Hierarchy asserted where the text itself names a contrary ranking**. Guard: flag only when the contrary ranking is on the page; no reception speculation.
- **Locus collision**: quantity in one span and quality in another, for opposite conclusions.

### 5. Editorial advice class
"Does your most vivid passage support your central claim or a side claim?"; "Make the ranking explicit where a reader who ranks differently would stop." The editor never supplies the ranking.

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
Presence reads as anecdote without data, and hierarchies read as missing warrants. The second is partly right: a hierarchy is a warrant, and the engine's warrant detection can host it with the right severity (an evaluative, recoverable warrant).

### 7. Classification
**A.** Hierarchies and loci are warrant types the engine already reconstructs; presence needs only the shared-premise and narrative rules to stop "missing evidence" findings on non-quantified spans.

## 11. Dissociation

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
Dissociation (*New Rhetoric* §§89–93) splits a previously unified notion into a devalued "appearance" term and a valued "reality" term, producing a change in the argument's conceptual basis. It has the most developed evaluative literature of Perelman's techniques: van Rees (2009) gives a book-length pragma-dialectical study with textual indicators, and an ISSA 2006 paper states soundness conditions (procedural: the dissociation is put up for discussion recognizably; material: the antagonist could accept the distinction), naming essentialist definitions immune to criticism and persuasive definitions as fallacious forms. Gata (ISSA 2010) finds that dissociated terms without clear definitions carry reduced argumentative weight. Schiappa's review criticizes the pragma-dialectical frame as limited. "Effective" means reframing the dispute. Evidence: theoretical and analytic; no effect sizes. Wilson (2018) connects dissociation to Douglass's jeremiad, and the Gettysburg dispute (Wills's "refounding" against Kendall's "swindle," Jaffa denying anything was hidden) is dissociation of a founding referent.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
"Real," "true," "genuine," "so-called," "merely," "nominal"; scare quotes; paired terms (letter/spirit, legal/just, security/safety); redefinition of a referent (a founding date, "the nation").

### 3. Success criteria
The dissociation is introduced visibly, gives a criterion for which term is the real one, is used consistently afterward, and is plausibly acceptable to the stated audience.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Equivocating dissociation**: the criterion is never stated or shifts. Guard: a one-off flourish with no downstream inferential work is not a finding.
- **Persuasive definition**: a term redefined while keeping its approval charge. Guard: a stipulation announced as a stipulation is fine.

### 5. Editorial advice class
"Name the criterion that makes this term the real one"; "use the dissociated term consistently once introduced." The editor never supplies the criterion.

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
It reads dissociation as scope drift or CL4 definitional migration and demands evidence for "real X," missing that the move proposes a conceptual starting point. CL4's existing exemption for lens essays (migration is the method; it fires only when concealed and spent at a cash-out) is the right model.

### 7. Classification
**A.** A named sub-check (criterion stated, used consistently, persuasive definition) inside the existing CL4 and definition analysis; stance triage does not cover it because it is not overstatement.

## 12. Enthymeme and shared premises

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
Rapp's reading of Aristotle treats the enthymeme as argument from audience-accepted premises (endoxa), with the "fewer premises" passage as advice about selection, which displaces the truncated-syllogism reading. Bitzer (1959) stresses that the audience supplies the missing material, making persuasion partly self-persuasion; Walker (2000) defines the enthymeme as a structure of inference and affect that caps a development. "Effective" means the audience completes the inference and owns the conclusion. The empirical record (O'Keefe meta-analyses) cuts against suppressing **conclusions**: explicit conclusions beat omitted ones (r ≈ .10, 17 studies); more specific and complete support persuades more (r ≈ .10, 18 studies; r ≈ .14); refutational two-sided messages beat one-sided ones (r ≈ .08, 42 studies), while non-refutational two-sided messages do worse (r ≈ −.05, 65 studies). The case for enthymeme therefore rests on suppressing shared **premises**; no empirical work on implicit premises specifically was found. Mixed evidence.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
Connectives with an unstated bridge ("so," "of course," "which is why"); rhetorical questions with assumed answers; "we all know"; maxims; a capping sentence after a development.

### 3. Success criteria
The suppressed premise is recoverable and belongs to the named or implied audience's commitments; any omitted conclusion is unambiguous; the capping turn follows from the development.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Suppressed premise the named audience rejects**. Guard: flag only when the text names an audience whose stated commitments differ or the premise is contested within the text.
- **Load-bearing factual, causal, or quantified suppressed premise**. Never exempt.
- **Ambiguous implicit conclusion** (two readers would finish it differently). Guard: in AT5, AT6, or witness spans, an implicit conclusion is not automatically a defect; apply "state it" advice only where the piece advances a standpoint (AT2, AT3).

### 5. Editorial advice class
"Which premise is the reader supplying here, and does your stated audience hold it?"; "If this premise is factual, it carries burden even unstated"; for standpoint pieces, note that the evidence favors stating the conclusion and refuting (beyond merely mentioning) the main objection.

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
WR0 fires on every enthymeme. That is the correct reconstruction step and the wrong severity default; the engine's existing EXPLICIT / RECOVERABLE / MISSING / CONTESTED scale already caps RECOVERABLE at Should-Fix, so the fix is a further cap for evaluative premises. Conversely, the engine's objection-handling demand is empirically supported, which is worth keeping and saying.

### 7. Classification
**C.** An unstated warrant that is evaluative or definitional, recoverable, and attributable to the named audience produces no finding unless contested in the text; factual, causal, and quantified premises never qualify. The O'Keefe finding that a mentioned but unrefuted objection does worse than none sharpens an existing AT3 rule (A-level).

## 13. Exemplum, illustration, and analogy

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
Aristotle treats example as rhetoric's induction: placed after enthymemes an example works like a witness and one suffices; placed first, several are needed (via a secondary source). Perelman & Olbrechts-Tyteca separate example (§78, establishes a rule), illustration (§79, strengthens adherence to a rule already accepted), and model and anti-model (§§80–81). Walton, Reed & Macagno (2008) treat analogy as defeasible with four critical questions (is the claim true in the source case; are the cases similar; do relevant differences undermine the similarity; is there a counter-case). Kienpointner's analysis of about 100 political figurative analogies adds parameters (domain distance, burden placement, didactic value, seriousness). Metaphor framing: Thibodeau & Boroditsky (2011) found crime-as-beast versus crime-as-virus shifted policy preference; Steen, Reijnierse & Burgers (2014, four experiments with a non-metaphorical control) did not replicate; Thibodeau & Boroditsky (2015) reported continued effects; Brugman, Burgers & Vis (2019; 91 studies, N=34,783) found positive effects, substantially larger for concept-level framing than single-word framing (pooled size not retrieved). "Effective" means adherence to a generalization, transfer of a judgment, or a reframe. Evidence: strong normative theory; moderate-to-thin and contested empirics, with single-word metaphor effects fragile.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
Placement of the case relative to the general claim (before it: inductive; after it: illustrative); similarity markers ("just as," "the same logic"); a stated basis of comparison; an extended metaphor across the piece versus a single word; whether the source case's own judgment is contested.

### 3. Success criteria
Example: the piece says or shows why the case is representative. Illustration: the rule is independently established or shared, and the case is apt. Analogy: the shared feature that matters is named and the most obvious disanalogy is addressed. Model: the imitable feature is specified.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Hasty generalization**. Guard: if the general claim is supported elsewhere and the case follows it, treat it as illustration; no finding.
- **False analogy** (relevant difference overwhelms similarity). Guard: a figurative analogy with no inferential load (humor, clarification) does not get the full critical questions.
- **Metaphor smuggling** a causal claim. Guard: flag only when the frame's entailment becomes an explicit premise for a prescription.

### 5. Editorial advice class
Decide whether a case is an example or an illustration and place it accordingly; name the basis of comparison; address the most obvious disanalogy; make sure a working metaphor's implied causal claim is supported or not relied on.

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
It flags every illustrative case as a hasty generalization, treats load-bearing analogies as decoration, and misses that an extended metaphor may be the piece's warrant. The engine's lens-essay note already calibrates WR0 on analogy spans; that should generalize beyond AT5.

### 7. Classification
**C.** The example/illustration distinction is a span-level applicability rule (illustration gets aptness checks, example gets representativeness checks), and analogy gets Walton's critical questions as its warrant check inside existing WR analysis.

## 14. Satire and irony

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
Booth's stable irony is intended, covert, stable, and finite: readers reject the surface, try alternatives, judge the author's beliefs, and settle on a meaning consonant with them, guided by clues (titles, proclaimed known error, internal contradiction, clashes of style). "Effective" means shared judgment of a target and reduced counterarguing; more reliably, attention and entertainment. The empirical record is substantial and the persuasion case is weak: Burgers & Brugman (2022; 70 studies, N=22,969) found large entertainment effects, a medium increase in message discounting, and limited evidence for a positive persuasion main effect; Boukes et al. (2015) found absorption reduced counterarguing, with effects conditional on prior agreement; Droog & Burgers (2024) found opposing mechanisms that suppress net effects. Misreading is documented: in LaMarre, Landreville & Beam (2009; N reported as 322 or 332), conservatives more often read Colbert as meaning his statements. **Demoted in the value ranking: the editorial value is in the reconstructability check, since the persuasion case is limited.**

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
Incongruity between voice and known facts; overstatement past plausibility; register clash; framing devices (title, persona byline); a nameable target.

### 3. Success criteria
One nameable target; at least one unmistakable clue that the surface is not the author's view; a finite reconstructed meaning statable in one sentence; any factual claim inside the reconstruction is one the writer would defend literally.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **No stable target** (reconstruction yields opposite readings). Guard: deliberate unstable irony in a writer-confirmed lens essay is not a defect.
- **Clue-free irony in a high-stakes genre** (testimony, regulatory comment): inherits the high-stakes rule.
- **Smuggled facts**: invented quotations or statistics read literally. Guard: labeled parody is not fabrication; fabricated attributions to real persons are never exempt.

### 5. Editorial advice class
"In one sentence, what does a reader who gets the joke conclude, and does every clue point there?"; "Where is the first unmistakable signal?"; "If a hostile reader quoted this paragraph literally, what would they quote?"

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
It extracts the surface claim as C0 and flags overstatement or strawman. Stance triage can earn ironic overstatement (S2 earned-by-frame), but it does not test reconstructability, which is the satire-specific check.

### 7. Classification
**A.** Stance triage plus a reconstruction precondition (target nameable, clue present, reconstruction finite) before an ironic span can be earned; reconstructed claims then carry burden at their own type.

## 15. Genealogy (Foucault and successors)

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
Genealogy traces how a concept or institution came to be in order to show contingency where necessity was assumed (*Discipline and Punish* is the canonical model). Its force varies by type: debunking (Nietzsche), vindicatory (Williams 2002), and problematizing (Koopman 2013, who argues Foucauldian genealogy escapes the genetic-fallacy charge because it does not derive normative conclusions directly from history). Srinivasan (2019) argues direct epistemic debunking is weaker than often supposed and reads genealogy's force through "genealogical luck" and what representations do. "Effective" means a reframed debate in which a category becomes open to revision. Evidence: philosophical; no persuasion studies found.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
Historical narration of a term's or institution's emergence; "it was not always so"; dated origin episodes; attention to who benefited; historical sources quoted against present usage.

### 3. Success criteria
Historical claims are accurate and sourced; the piece is explicit about its cash-out: contingency (it could be otherwise), function (it serves X), or invalidity (it is unjustified), and an invalidity cash-out supplies a present-tense reason beyond origin.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Genetic fallacy**: origin offered as sufficient proof of invalidity. Guard: no finding when the cash-out is contingency or function, or when present-tense reasons are added.
- **Selective history**. Guard: flag only when the text makes a "this was always about X" totality claim.
- **Vindicatory overreach**: a flattering origin used to immunize a practice (the symmetrical case).
- **Origin–present gap**: no account of continuity between original purpose and present function.

### 5. Editorial advice class
"Which of the three cash-outs is this paragraph making?"; "If invalidity, what present reason supports it?"; "Is each historical claim sourced?"

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
It reads the history as evidence for an implicit evaluative C0 and fires a missing warrant (correct for an invalidity cash-out, a false positive for problematization), or it reads the history as AT1 background and misses the evaluative work.

### 7. Classification
**A.** AT5 already states the Koopman/Srinivasan picture (coherence and fertility, asserted burden at cash-outs); add a named cash-out type (contingency / function / invalidity) to the existing cash-out inventory.

## 16. Call-and-response and communal forms

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
Smitherman defines call-response as spontaneous verbal and non-verbal interaction in which listeners punctuate the speaker's calls with responses. Lischer's study of King describes it as a reciprocal energy exchange, with formulas and set pieces timed for delivery, and reports that the printed sermons flattened what the tapes preserve. Miller (1992) shows King merging his voice with sources from the white Protestant radio pulpit and the Black folk pulpit, which bears on attribution expectations in homiletic genres. Campbell's "feminine style" (1989) shares the participatory core: personal experience, invited participation, and a first-person plural that blurs rhetor and audience. "Effective" means co-performed meaning and solidarity. Evidence: criticism and ethnography; the effect depends on live delivery, and **no research was found on how written texts signal response to a silent reader**. Thin for the page.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
Anaphora and refrain; rhythmic parallelism; rhetorical questions with an expected collective answer; paragraphing or lineation that marks pauses; a response modeled in the text.

### 3. Success criteria
The refrain is short and stable enough to anticipate; the structure gives response slots a reader can supply from the text; delivery mode is known (spoken or read).

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Call without response space** on the page (irregular refrain, response never modeled). Guard: text written for delivery may legitimately read thin; ask whether it will be spoken.
- **Repetition as padding**. Guard: repetition in a confirmed participatory span is structure.

### 5. Editorial advice class
"Is the refrain stable enough for a silent reader to anticipate?"; "Is this text meant to be read aloud?"

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
Anaphora and refrain read as redundancy or scope drift; the insider audience reads as AC3 (preaching to the converted).

### 7. Classification
**C.** A rule that repetition and refrain spans produce no redundancy finding when the participatory function is confirmed, plus a delivery-mode question at intake; too narrow for its own row and housed under AT6.

## 17. Non-Western and Indigenous oratorical functions

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
The scholarship supports a small set of transferable **functions**, which the tool should recognize without cataloguing cultures: (a) consummatory function or ritual self-address, where the primary audience is the speaker's own community (Lake 1983 on Red Power; Black 2009 on mixed inward and outward function); (b) proverb or tradition as warrant (Yankah 1995 on Akan royal oratory; the pepeha and whakataukī of whaikōrero; Lu 1998 on analogy in early Chinese rhetoric); (c) indirection and surrogate speech that protect face and relationship (Yankah); (d) relational accountability through ancestry, land, and the dead before the business (whaikōrero mihi; the Haudenosaunee condolence ceremony opening councils); (e) tactical use of dominant genres (Powell 2002, "survivance"). Lyons's "rhetorical sovereignty" (2000), Indigenous peoples' capacity to determine their own textual representation, cuts against any tool that detects "Indigenous rhetoric" and coaches toward a reconstructed form. Comparative projects have been faulted for imposing Western categories (Wardy's review of Lu). "Effective" means identity formation, recognition, relational repair, and sovereignty over representation. Evidence: ethnographic and critical; the whaikōrero source is an educational resource for a specific ceremonial context. No experiments.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
A relational or ceremonial opening; proverbs or traditional sayings in warrant position; oblique demands; address to the dead or to ancestors; a primary audience that is the in-group.

### 3. Success criteria
The opening connects to what follows; the proverb fits the case for the audience that shares the tradition; indirection is decodable by its intended addressee; in an instrumental venue, an instrumental span exists alongside the consummatory one.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Undecodable indirection for the actual addressee** (testimony to a legislature that cannot read the reference). Guard: indirection aimed at an in-group is not a failure.
- **Consummatory text in an instrumental venue** with no instrumental span. Guard: suggest adding a marked instrumental span; never suggest stripping the consummatory one.
- **Borrowed form without the relationship**. Guard: raise fit and provenance as questions; the tool never decides who is entitled to a form.

### 5. Editorial advice class
Ask what function the opening, proverb, or indirection serves, and for which audience; check decodability for the named addressee; in high-stakes venues, ask where the instrumental ask lives.

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
It fires: no clear C0 (indirection), appeal to tradition (proverb), structural delay (relational opening), no objection engagement (self-address), and it assumes the addressee is the dominant public.

### 7. Classification
**C.** Applicability rules triggered only by writer confirmation (proverb warrants judged for fit and sharedness; relational openings not flagged as delay; no objection-handling on self-address; C0 extractability judged for the declared audience), housed with AT6; the tool never infers a writer's culture from text features.

## 18. Enactment (and consciousness-raising)

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
In enactment the speaker "incarnates the argument": the speaker's act or person is proof of what is said (Campbell & Jamieson 1978, as quoted by Corredor 2019, who extends it to claims supported by the activity of making them). Campbell's account of consciousness-raising (1973) shows personal problems revealed as political, though Hayden (2018) argues it does not explain how a choice-based process yields collective action. "Effective" means the act stands as proof of capacity, presence, or standing. Evidence: criticism only; thin.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
"I am here," "we are speaking," the speaker's identity or presence foregrounded as relevant; personal experience offered as a reason; first-person plural that includes the reader.

### 3. Success criteria
The act or identity is visible on the page, and the claim it supports is one the act can prove (capacity, existence of a constituency, standing).

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
- **Enactment mismatch**: the act proves A and is offered for B (being affected proves the effect on the speaker; it does not prove an aggregate effect). Guard: enactment does prove claims about capacity, presence, and standing; flag only when the claim is broader than the act.
- **Anecdote treated as weakness** (the engine's failure). Guard: flag only where an anecdote is sole support for a population-level claim.

### 5. Editorial advice class
"Your presence and standing prove A; the sentence claims B. Is B something your experience can establish?"

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
It reads "I am here, I am speaking" as irrelevant or as an appeal to experience, missing that some claims are proved by the act.

### 7. Classification
**A.** Enactment is testimony in which the act is the evidence, and the AT4 observational/representative split already captures its main failure; it needs only a note in the AT4 row that an act can discharge the observational burden.

## 19. Strategic maneuvering (design bridge)

### 1. What it does, what effective means, evidence
Van Eemeren's strategic maneuvering (2010; van Eemeren, Garssen & Meuffels 2015) names the arguer's continual effort to reconcile effectiveness with reasonableness through topical potential, audience demand, and presentational devices; a maneuver "derails" into fallacy only when it violates a critical-discussion rule in context. Walton, Reed & Macagno's argumentation schemes supply scheme-specific critical questions (example, analogy, sign, expert opinion, consequences). For this design problem, "effective" means a principled basis for applicability rules: a rhetorical move produces a finding only where it derails. Evidence: strong theoretical base, thin empirical base.

### 2. Page-visible moves and signals
Writers do not use it as a mode. Its signal belongs to the engine: any presentational or audience-adaptive move that does not, on its face, violate a rule.

### 3. Success criteria
Each applicability rule names the derailment condition under which the switched-off question switches back on.

### 4. Characteristic failures (with false-positive guards)
Adopting the derailment view is a substantive choice: Schiappa and Srinivasan both suggest some approaches resist evaluation by dialectical soundness at all. Guard: state the choice in documentation (see The critique).

### 5. Editorial advice class
None directly; it shapes how findings are worded ("this move works until X; at X it carries burden").

### 6. How the current engine misreads it
No misreading; the engine already cites Perelman and could cite pragma-dialectics to show it is broader than "Toulmin-plus-steelmanning."

### 7. Classification
**A.** A justification for the C rules below, adding no row and no subsystem.

## Combining modes

Strong persuasive texts rarely stay in one mode, and the notes converge on one finding about how they mix: **the seam is where diagnosis belongs**. Douglass's 1852 oration is the clearest case. On Terrill's reading, Douglass first invites his white audience into an ironic, multi-perspective spectatorship and then closes those vistas by calling for judgment and action; later he moves from epideictic irony into a forensic reading of the Constitution. The first seam (irony to exigence) is a constitutive and ethical turn; the second (indictment to legal interpretation) is where claim-and-support checks legitimately switch back on, because an interpretive legal claim now carries asserted burden. A tool that runs one burden across the whole speech will be wrong in one half of it whichever burden it picks.

The empirical work on combining narrative and statistics gives outcome-dependent answers, and it should not be averaged:

| Study | Outcome | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Allen et al. 2000 | persuasion index | combined (M=17.91) > statistical (17.58) > narrative (17.32) > none (17.22); differences small |
| Allen & Preiss 1997 (k=16, N=1,836) | persuasion | r=.074 favoring statistical |
| Zebregs et al. 2015 | beliefs, attitude, intention | statistics stronger on beliefs and attitude; narrative stronger on intention |
| Small, Loewenstein & Slovic 2007 | donations | identifiable victim $2.38; statistical $1.14; identifiable plus statistics $1.43 |
| Kalla & Broockman 2020 (6,869 voters) | exclusionary attitudes, in conversation | arguments alone: no detectable change; adding narrative exchange: durable, d ≈ 0.08 |
| Coppock 2022 | policy attitudes, survey experiments | ordinary information moves attitudes about 5 points, similarly across subgroups, decaying over about 10 days |

The conflict between Small et al. and the belief-outcome studies is partly an artifact of what was measured (charitable giving against belief change), and Kalla & Broockman against Coppock is partly conversation against survey. The engine cannot know uptake, but it can ask the writer which outcome a span seeks (belief, action, recognition) and check placement against that answer. That is a question, never a prediction.

**Seam failures that are page-visible**, each with the existing code that should carry it:

- **Unmarked mode switch where a claim relies on the lower burden** (a story slides into a causal claim that borrows the story's force). This is the AT0 pattern at span level; a signaled switch is not AT0.
- **Statistic and story describe different populations**, or the text never says whether the case is typical, exceptional, or "the face of the number." Existing NE2 (vignette-claim mismatch) covers it.
- **Cash-out drift**: the explicit claim after a narrative or witness span asks for more than the span showed. Existing NE1 and BP6 cover it once they fire at the cash-out.
- **Fact insertion inside an address span**: a quantified or causal claim placed in a constitutive or solidarity passage, where the surrounding exemption tempts the reader (and the engine) to wave it through. This is exactly what the constructed pair tests.
- **Concealed cash-out**: a prescription delivered at full force under invitational, lens, or witness cover. Existing GN2 is the model and should apply beyond AT5.

**What this section smooths over.** The literatures behind these modes do not share a concept of "effective." Fisher means adherence grounded in good reasons; the meta-analyses mean small, short-term attitude shifts in mostly health and consumer experiments; Fricker and Dotson mean just uptake of a speaker as a knower; testimonio scholars mean representation of a collective; Charland means a formed subject. An editor optimizing for absorption (keep the story uninterrupted) may work against a witness's epistemic standing, which is not supposed to depend on how gripping the story is, and Green & Brock's fact/fiction result cuts against reading a story's force as evidence of its truth. Seam diagnosis manages these conflicts locally; it does not resolve them.

## Recognition

The tool should **propose, the writer should confirm**, on the model the engine already uses for AT5, and recognition should operate on spans as well as documents. Cheap page signals that justify a proposal:

| Approach | Signals that justify proposing it |
|---|---|
| Constitutive / AT6 | named collective; heavy "we/you" relative to claims; shared-past narration ending in a handed role; a stated refusal to argue |
| Witness (AT4) | first-person access markers; I-to-we shifts; quoted or recorded speech of a named person |
| Narrative | identifiable protagonist; sequential particulars; a "this is why" cash-out |
| Jeremiad / prophetic | founding text invoked; catalog of betrayals; conditional future or crisis time |
| Invitational | freedom clauses; perspective owned as one's own; no or subordinated ask |
| Agonistic | named adversary; contested frame stated; explicit refusal of consensus |
| Illustration / analogy | case placement after a supported rule; "just as"; extended metaphor |
| Satire / irony | register clash; implausible overstatement; persona byline |
| Genealogy | origin narration of a term or institution; "it was not always so" |
| Participatory | anaphora, refrains, questions with collective answers; delivery intended |
| Relational / consummatory functions | ceremonial or relational opening; proverbs in warrant position; in-group primary audience |

Four intake questions do most of the work and cost little: **Who is the primary audience** (in-group, adversary, decision-maker, general public)? **What should the reader be, recognize, or do at the end?** **Will it be spoken?** **Does any quoted speech come through a recorder, translator, or composite?**

Mislabeling runs in three directions, and each needs its own guard. (1) **The status quo error**: an alternative approach scored as failed claim-and-support. This is what the change fixes. (2) **The gaming error**: a writer (or the engine) claims "constitutive" or "witness" to escape burden. Guard: labels change which questions apply and never the burden on claims; under the high-stakes override every claim anywhere is a cash-out; GN2-style concealed-cash-out findings run under every label. (3) **The under-diagnosis error**: a claim-and-support text with a stirring opening is relabeled AT6 and its real argument goes unaudited. Guard: AT6 is assigned to spans, and any span containing an assertion is inventoried as a cash-out. A fourth risk is specific to relational and Indigenous functions: inferring a writer's culture from text features. The tool must never do this; it recognizes a function the writer confirms (Lyons's rhetorical sovereignty), and it never coaches toward a reconstructed traditional form. Swanson's point that deliberately ambiguous slogans can unify readers who hold incompatible interpretations names a pattern the tool cannot reliably detect; it belongs in documentation as a known limit.

| AT4 | Testimonial | "I witnessed this; here's what it means" | Split: **observational** (low; may be discharged by the speaker's act or presence for claims about capacity, presence, or standing), **interpretive** (high), **representative** (sample, role, or a **named mandate**), **attribution** (for recorded, translated, reconstructed, or composite speech: speaker, recorder, language chain, date of record against event, recorder's interest) |
| AT5 | Generative / lens | "Look through this lens" | Split: coherence and fertility; asserted burden at each cash-out (cash-out types now include contingency / function / invalidity for genealogy) |
| **AT6** | **Constitutive / relational address** | "Here is who we are, and what that asks of us" / "I address you; recognize this" | Split: **address coherence** (the collective is named, its scope stable or visibly widened; the implied auditor is one the stated audience can occupy; exclusions are owned), **closure** (the text hands the reader a role or act, or marks recognition as its end), **cash-out** (every factual, causal, quantified, historical, attributive, or prescriptive claim at asserted burden). Objection handling does not apply to address spans. Writer-confirmed, never silently inferred. |

### Finding rules

1. **Typed C0.** Step 2 records C0 as **ASSERTION** (default) or **ADDRESS**. An address C0 has the form "the text asks [named audience] to be / recognize / do [X]," and is available only under writer-confirmed AT6 or a witness text whose stated purpose is to be heard. CL0 still fires when neither form can be stated. (The engine's own Step 9 decision table already phrases claim-accessibility as "main claim or worldview," and its personal-essay and testimony notes already allow an implicit ladder; this rule makes that explicit.)
2. **Verdict placement for an address C0.** The document-level Step 9 warrant verdict is reported as "address C0; verdicts at cash-outs" and is not computed. Each cash-out receives its own WARRANTED / UNCONVENTIONAL-BUT-WARRANTED / UNWARRANTED verdict. An AT6 coherence failure is Should-Fix by default; Must-Fix requires CL0 (no statable address) or a defeated cash-out.
3. **Span functions switch questions off, never burdens.** A span may carry a confirmed function: ADDRESS, OBSERVATION, NARRATIVE, ILLUSTRATION, SHARED-PREMISE, IRONIC, RELATIONAL-OPENING, PARTICIPATORY. Inside it, these questions do not apply and produce no finding: objection handling and rhetorical fairness (OB), WR0 on the span's internal moves, redundancy, hasty generalization (illustration only), structural delay (relational opening), register or tone. Every switched-off question switches back on at the span's cash-out.
4. **Cash-out inventory beyond AT5.** Any document with function-tagged spans receives AT5's cash-out inventory. Every factual, causal, quantified, historical, attributive, representative, or prescriptive sentence is a cash-out at asserted burden, wherever it sits. Under the high-stakes override, every claim in the document is a cash-out.
5. **Stance triage cannot earn a fact.** The existing block on earning prescriptions extends to factual, causal, quantified, and attribution claims and to dated or quantified predictions. Ironic overstatement is earnable only after a reconstruction test passes (target nameable, clue present, reconstructed meaning statable in one sentence).
6. **Shared premises.** An unstated warrant that is evaluative, definitional, or traditional (a value hierarchy, a covenant, a proverb), recoverable, and attributable to the declared audience produces no finding unless the text itself contests it. Empirical premises framed as shared keep DI0 and AC1 exactly as now.
7. **Example, illustration, analogy.** A case that follows an independently supported or shared rule is an illustration and gets aptness checks only; a case that precedes or solely supports a rule is an example and gets representativeness checks (NE1, BP6). Analogies that carry inferential load get Walton's four critical questions as their WR check; figurative analogies without inferential load do not. Dissociations get a sub-check under CL4: criterion stated, used consistently, persuasive definition announced or concealed.
8. **AT4 parity and disclosure.** Observational spans are held to one standard regardless of the speaker's social position, and corroboration is requested only where an observation is load-bearing for an interpretive, causal, quantified, or representative claim. An undisclosed composite or pseudonymous case presented as a single real person is Must-Fix under any label. Quoted speech that reaches the writer through a recorder or translator carries the attribution burden; quoting it as a reception artifact lowers that burden.
9. **Purpose-specific replacements for OB.** Under confirmed agonistic purpose, the steelman question is replaced by an adversary-recognizability check (would they recognize their position?). Under confirmed invitational purpose, it is replaced by a purpose-coherence check (does any ask contradict the stated freedom?), reported through GN2. Under self-address or solidarity purpose, nothing replaces it.
10. **Seams.** AT0 does not fire on a signaled mode switch. It fires when a switch is unsignaled and a claim after the switch relies on the burden of the span before it. The open-letter genre default should be "AT3 or AT6, confirm at intake" (the installed reference currently defaults open letters and manifestos to AT3).

Rules 1–2 are the only change touching `Argument_State`: one enumerated field on C0 and one permitted value on the document verdict. Everything else is a row, a split, or an applicability rule.

### Classification summary and value ranking

No approach needs **D**. The strongest D candidate is the constructed solidarity address (below): a text with no propositional thesis and no policy claim. Under rules 1–4 it receives an address C0, coherence checks, and zero argument findings; its variant receives exactly one finding at the inserted sentence. The second candidate, a heavily mode-mixed text such as Douglass, is handled by span functions and the seam rule, using the span mechanism AT5 cash-outs already introduced.

### Agreement and disagreement with Source 1

This source agrees with Source 1 that an AT6 relational/constitutive row, an AT4 extension, and applicability rules cover the field, and that nothing else needs new architecture. It disagrees on the one schema change Source 1 proposes. Source 1 would let `Argument_State` hold a text with no global C0 or global warrant verdict; this source would keep C0 mandatory and type it, while agreeing that the document-level warrant verdict should not be computed for an address. The reasons: a solidarity address has a statable point (what it asks its audience to be or do), the engine's claim-accessibility test already accepts a "worldview," and a C0-optional schema would remove CL0's ability to separate a deliberate address from a text that never commits to anything. The two positions converge on where verdicts live (at cash-outs) and differ on whether the empty slot or the typed slot is the leaner and safer representation; this source judges the typed slot cheaper because it changes one field instead of making a required one optional across every consumer of `Argument_State`.

## Guardrails

**Never exempt, under any label, span function, or stance verdict:** factual, causal, quantified, historical, attributive, and prescriptive claims, and dated or quantified predictions. This list was reached independently in all five research passes. The label diagnoses form; it never certifies legitimacy, and the constitutive and identification literatures themselves document their mechanisms serving exclusion and deception (Charland's illusion of freedom; the *peithō* and *dolos* distinction; organizational identification as unobtrusive control; Burke's reading of *Mein Kampf*).

**Propaganda and bad faith.** Four checks stay in the diagnostic lane because each is a question the text answers about itself:

1. **Ideal withdrawal** (from Stanley's account of undermining propaganda as discourse presented as embodying an ideal while tending to erode it): does the text invoke an ideal (equality, the people, freedom) while its own prescriptions or factual claims, read literally, withdraw that ideal from a group it names? This asks for coherence; it is not a verdict on the cause.
2. **Accurate facts are not clearance** (Ellul: information is an essential element of propaganda). Fact-checking passes do not end the review.
3. **Out-group attributions carry burden** (Burke): claims about an opponent's motives, crimes, intentions, or numbers are factual or causal claims.
4. **The adversary/enemy line runs always**: denial of standing, dehumanization, and essentializing attributions are findings under any label. Anger, heat, and incivility are not. Mouffe's framework itself condemns the slide, so the check is internal to agonism.

Chambers's distinction between deliberative rhetoric (engages judgment) and plebiscitary rhetoric (bypasses it) is the principled line under all four, and the documentation should name it as the line.

**Fabricated and composite testimony.** The cases (Cooke's invented child in 1980; Finkel's composite in 2001; Frey's memoir in 2003–06; Seltzer's invented identity in 2008; and the contested Menchú case) share one pattern: the fabrication sits in the particulars that carried the persuasive load. Journalism codes already draw the line the engine needs. The SPJ Code requires labeling illustrations and re-enactments and never distorting facts or context; the NYT guidelines refrain from assigning fictional names, ages, places, or dates and require any shielding device to be disclosed, and they treat every word between quotation marks as the speaker's own. The engine's rule is **disclosure**, and it prohibits no device: a disclosed composite becomes a representative claim (AT4 handles it); an undisclosed one is Must-Fix. For Menchú-type texts, observational and representative burdens are reported separately, and success on one never covers the other in either direction.

**Emotional appeals.** Following Walton on appeal to pity, an emotional appeal inside a witness or narrative span is not a finding; it becomes one when offered as a reason for a conclusion it is not relevant to, especially a prescription, so the relevance test runs at the cash-out. Walton's analysis of the Gulf War incubator testimony shows why the emotional check and the provenance check must run together. Small et al. caution against advising writers to "add statistics" to a narrative by reflex; the editor's question is whether the representative claim is supported somewhere.

**Process guardrails.** Labels are writer-confirmed; the tool proposes. The high-stakes override is unchanged and governs burden level (every claim a cash-out); it does not reactivate switched-off questions on spans that contain no claims. Overrides of any of the above are recorded through the existing Deficit Lock and Severity Calibration path, never silently. No output may rank causes, judge which democratic theory is right, or predict audience uptake.

## The critique

**What the critique gets right, and what this recommendation answers.** Its strongest point is form bias: Young's internal exclusion and Sanders's observation that deliberation requires unequally distributed resources both show that a single ideal of argument can misclassify legitimate contributions as defective. A tool that tells a witness or constitutive writer only what they failed to prove reproduces that exclusion. Rules 1–4 answer it: the engine gains a positive account of what these texts do (AT6's coherence and closure burdens, AT4's mandate and parity rules) and stops scoring their non-asserting spans against assertion norms. The second fair point is purpose bias. The steelman norm presupposes that the goal is agreement with an opponent, and agonism, consummatory self-address, and invitational rhetoric each reject that goal on different grounds; rule 9 replaces the steelman question with purpose-specific checks. The third is audience bias: the engine's default reader is a neutral skeptical outsider, and the intake question about primary audience answers it.

**Where the critique overreaches.** Its picture of "Toulmin-plus-steelmanning" is narrower than the field the engine draws on. Perelman, already in the engine's grounding, is an audience-relative rhetoric; pragma-dialectics theorizes effectiveness within reasonableness (strategic maneuvering); Garsten defends a politics of persuasion that admits passion and partiality; and Chambers and Dryzek accept rhetoric into deliberative systems while keeping the line between rhetoric that engages judgment and rhetoric that bypasses it. Johnson's dialectical tier gives a principled reason, more than a parochial habit, to require objection handling when a text asserts or prescribes to others. The empirical record also supports the engine's norms for asserting spans: O'Keefe's meta-analyses find explicit conclusions, articulated support, and refutation of objections modestly more persuasive, with mere acknowledgment of objections worse than none. Those experiments measure belief change; they do not measure mobilization, identity, or reframing, which is exactly where the alternative approaches claim their effects, so the data defend the engine within its home territory and say little beyond it. Lozano-Reich and Cloud's critique of invitational rhetoric (it presupposes an equality that does not exist) applies equally to a deliberative ideal that presupposes equal standing, which suggests the critique's real force is power sensitivity, and power sensitivity is not a general preference for alternative forms.

**Honest disagreements to state in documentation.**

1. The tool keeps the commitment that factual, causal, quantified, and prescriptive claims bear burdens in every genre. Agonists and some difference democrats would call that liberal-rationalist. The tool should say it is a deliberate choice grounded in the engages-versus-bypasses line, and say so plainly.
2. Mapping alternative approaches onto applicability rules adopts van Eemeren's "derailment" view: a rhetorical move is fine until it violates a rule in context. Srinivasan's non-epistemic reading of genealogy and Schiappa's critique of pragma-dialectics suggest some approaches resist evaluation by dialectical soundness at all. This is a defensible choice, and a contestable one.
3. AT6 groups approaches with different ethical valences: Charland's constitution can be coercive, Young's greeting is about recognizing difference, and Burke's identification can operate unconsciously. One row treats them as one family for burden purposes; the documentation should say the grouping is functional and implies no endorsement.
4. The tool cannot judge whether an interpellation will take with a real audience. Inhabitability of the implied auditor is a proxy, and an imperfect one.

## Test-text expectations

Each note below is written as if the text were a draft submitted by its author, except where provenance makes the relevant writer someone quoting it. "Would say" and "would not say" are the diagnostic expectations a merged rubric can test against.

### Frederick Douglass, "What to the Slave Is the Fourth of July?" (Rochester, July 5, 1852)

**Approaches used.** African American jeremiad (covenant, declension, closing hope; Howard-Pitney; Bell; Wilson); epideictic rearticulation of the founders and of the oration genre itself (Jasinski); managed irony that turns into exigence (Terrill); dissociation (Wilson); constitutive work through pronouns, with distancing second-person possessives held in tension with his address to fellow citizens (Fulkerson counts 33 distancing pronouns in the historical narration, Stephens 45); prophetic indictment; and a forensic span on the Constitution. The text announces its method: "scorching irony, not convincing argument, is needed."

**What a strong note would say.** It would record the moral claim (slavery is wrong) as deliberately unargued: the refusal is the rhetorical point, so no CL, WR, or OB finding attaches to it. It would ask whether the pivot from irony to direct address is marked clearly enough to carry the audience across it. It would treat the Constitution passage as a mode switch into interpretive-legal argument at asserted burden (this was contested in its own time, with the Garrisonians holding the opposite reading) and ask whether the support Douglass gestures at is sufficient for the claim as stated. It would check the factual claims at ordinary burden: the Fugitive Slave Act commissioner's fees (ten dollars and five) match §8 of the 1850 Act; his figure of one-seventh of the population matches the 1850 census (3,204,313 enslaved of 23,191,876, about 1 in 7.2); the figure of seventy-two capital crimes for enslaved people in Virginia should be sourced (unverified in this research).

**What it would not say.** It would not ask Douglass to steelman slaveholders, add objection handling on the morality of slavery, or soften the second-person indictment; it would not issue a Must-Fix for his statement that nothing here needs arguing; it would not take a position on whether Douglass was "always a patriot" (Boxill) or on whether the antislavery reading of the Constitution is correct.

### Sojourner Truth, Akron Women's Rights Convention (May 29, 1851): the transcription problem

**Approaches used.** In Marius Robinson's report (*Anti-Slavery Bugle*, June 21, 1851), reviewed with Truth before publication: witness grounded in bodily labor and enactment (her body and work as proof of capacity), biblical exegesis resting on scriptural premises the audience shared (enthymeme), a constitutive self-naming ("I am a woman's rights"), and a closing satiric figure of men caught between a hawk and a buzzard. The famous refrain, the Southern dialect, the thirteen children sold away, and the hostile crowd come from Frances Dana Gage's 1863 version, published twelve years later; Truth was raised speaking Dutch in upstate New York, had about five children with one sold away, and Gage's own 1851 description of the audience was largely friendly. Painter judged Robinson's report the more reliable.

**What a strong note would say.** For a writer quoting the speech, it would ask which witness text is being used and apply the AT4 attribution sub-burden: quoting Gage's refrain as Truth's verbatim words is an attribution finding at ordinary burden, separate from a representation problem (an imposed dialect). For the Robinson text, the self-reports of labor are observational testimony at low burden, and the biblical argument is a shared-premise enthymeme judged for fit.

**What it would not say.** It would not credit the anaphoric refrain to Truth's craft (that craft is Gage's); it would not adjudicate what Truth "really meant"; it would not treat Gage's version as worthless, since it remains a significant artifact of reception and can be cited as such at a lower burden.

### The Gettysburg Address (November 19, 1863)

**Approaches used.** Epideictic funeral oration; constitutive refounding of the nation around the Declaration (Wills); dissociation of the founding referent through the dating to 1776 instead of 1787–89; closure-by-action handed to the living; an epideictic humility topos phrased as a prediction ("The world will little note, nor long remember what we say here").

**What a strong note would say.** It would record an address C0 (the living are asked to take up the dead's unfinished cause) with no document-level warrant verdict. It would treat the dating as the interpretive lens itself, judged for coherence and consistency with the redefinition it serves, with no demand for evidence. It would ask any writer quoting the text to name the manuscript or transcription, since the words *under God* are absent from the Nicolay and Hay copies, present in the Everett, Bancroft, and Bliss copies and in three reporters' accounts, and the reading copy is disputed (the Library of Congress favors Nicolay, the American Battlefield Trust leans toward Hay).

**What it would not say.** It would not ask for evidence that the nation will be reborn in freedom, and it would not flag the humility line as an unsupported forecast: the test for a prediction is whether it is quantified, dated, or load-bearing for a prescription, and this one is a conventional figure. It would describe the refounding as a dissociation without labeling it legitimate or a swindle; the Wills, Kendall, and Jaffa dispute is the guardrail tension in miniature, and the tool stays out of it.

### Seneca Falls Declaration of Sentiments (July 19–20, 1848)

**Approaches used.** Constitutive appropriation of the Declaration of Independence ("all men and women are created equal," with *He* in place of King George); identification through a borrowed canonical template; anaphora (*He has...*); eighteen grievances, which are factual claims about law and custom; eleven resolutions, which are prescriptions (only the franchise resolution was not unanimous).

**What a strong note would say.** It would treat the template as an earned constitutive and parodic frame and test each grievance at ordinary factual burden, because the grievances are the support for the resolutions. Franchise and coverture claims are broadly accurate for 1848. The property claim is overbroad as stated given New York's Married Women's Property Act of April 7, 1848 (wages are a separate question the research did not resolve). The claim that all colleges were closed to women is overstated given Oberlin's admission of women (with bachelor's degrees by 1841). Rule 5 applies: the parody frame cannot earn factual overstatement. The resolutions carry AT3 burden, discharged mainly through the grievances. A writer using Seneca Falls as the movement's origin would be pointed to Tetrault's account of how that origin story was later constructed.

**What it would not say.** It would not object that *He* personifies a whole sex as a tyrant (stance earned by frame) or ask the authors to engage the strongest case for coverture; it would not issue any verdict on the cause.

### Chief Joseph's surrender speech (Bear's Paw, October 5, 1877): the provenance problem

**Approaches used.** Witness and lament; enactment (the surrender as the act); relational address to his own leaders. The text reached print through a chain: Joseph to the messengers Captain John and Old George, through the interpreter Arthur Chapman, heard by Howard and Miles, written down by Lt. C. E. S. Wood, who later said he took it down for his own benefit "as a literary item." Wood published variant renderings from 1877 to 1939 (Venn counts about 19 published versions overall), and the penciled original is lost. Aoki concluded the message was likely embellished by Wood and does not exemplify American Indian oratory; Moulton suggests Joseph conveyed the meaning even if not the words.

**What a strong note would say.** It would separate approach from provenance. For a writer quoting the text as Joseph's words, the AT4 attribution sub-burden applies: the repair is a hedge naming the transmission (for example, as recorded by Wood), never deletion. Used as a reception artifact ("the words attributed to Joseph that became the canonical surrender speech"), the burden drops. Its observational content (named leaders dead, the cold, children missing) is checkable against the battle record in Greene. A writer seeking Joseph's own argument about broken promises could be pointed to the better-attested 1879 *North American Review* article (itself interpreted and edited) as a comparison of provenance strength.

**What it would not say.** It would not declare the speech authentic or fake. It would not praise "Joseph's oratorical technique" or read its cadence as Indigenous oratorical form, since that craft may be Wood's, and the tool never infers a cultural form from text features.

### Constructed pair (test fixture)

**Fixture A, constructed (solidarity address, no policy claim; fictional, about 110 words):**

> To the night-shift workers of Harbor Street: we see you. We know the hours you keep while the city sleeps, the buses you wait for in the dark, the meals eaten standing up. Some of you came here last year; some of you have worked these floors for twenty. Tonight we are not here to ask anything of anyone in the building behind us. We are here to stand beside you, to say your names aloud, and to promise that when you walk out at dawn you will not walk alone. You are the people who keep this city running. We will be here next week, and the week after.

**Fixture B, constructed:** the same text with one sentence inserted after "you will not walk alone": *Within two years, standing together like this will raise night-shift pay on this street by thirty percent.*

**Approaches used.** Both: constitutive address (named collective, hailing "you are the people who..."), Young's greeting, identification by sympathy, enactment (presence as proof of solidarity), invitational restraint toward the building's occupants. B adds one quantified causal prediction.

**What a strong note on A would say.** AT6 proposed for confirmation; address C0 = the text asks the night-shift workers to recognize themselves as seen and accompanied, and commits the speakers to return. No argument findings. At most two optional questions: who the "we" is (unnamed speakers are normal at a rally; the question matters only if the text will circulate in print), and whether the occupants of "the building behind us" should remain in the third person (a third-persona question, posed as a choice). "You are the people who keep this city running" is a constitutive hail and shared evaluative premise, and "we will be here next week" is a commitment the speakers can enact; neither is a forecast.

**What it would not say about A.** CL0, "no thesis," "no objections engaged," AC3 sympathetic-audience flattery, NE0 unattached vignette, or any request for data.

**What a strong note on B would say.** Exactly one finding, at the inserted sentence: an unsupported quantified causal prediction, carried at asserted burden (rule 4), which stance triage cannot earn (rule 5). Default severity Should-Fix, since the address C0 is unaffected and the claim does not defeat it; Must-Fix under a high-stakes setting or if the writer confirms the prediction as the text's central promise. The editor names the options (support it, qualify it, or remove it) and writes none of them. The note might add that the sentence is the only place the address makes a claim that could be checked against events, which is why it is the only place that draws a finding.

**What it would not say about B.** It would not reclassify the whole address as AT3, would not let the inserted sentence pull objection or warrant findings onto the surrounding address spans, and would not excuse the sentence because it sits inside a solidarity address. The pair passes only if A draws zero findings and B draws one.

## Confidence & gaps

**High confidence.** (1) The never-exempt list (factual, causal, quantified, historical, attributive, prescriptive claims, and predictions) was reached independently in all five research passes and matches the engine's existing block on earning prescriptions. (2) The structural diagnosis, that the main misreadings come from asking assertion questions of non-asserting spans, recurs across every approach and is consistent with the engine's own existing calibrations (AT1's optional objections, AT5's cash-out logic, the personal-essay and testimony genre notes). (3) No approach needs class D: the strongest candidate, a pure solidarity address, is fully specified by the typed-C0 and cash-out rules, and the constructed pair tests that claim directly.

**Moderate confidence.** (1) Whether AT6 should be its own row or a declared register of AT5. This source chose a row because the success criteria (inhabitable auditor, closure-by-action, owned exclusions) differ from lens fertility, but the AT5 machinery is reused almost entirely, and a merger would be defensible. (2) The typed-C0 proposal is this source's design inference, neither drawn from the literature nor tested; it rests on a reading of the installed Dialectical Clarity reference (its C0 extraction rule, CL0, the Step 9 decision table, and genre notes), and the PR #288 spec and Source 1 itself were not read, only the brief's one-line summary of Source 1. (3) The value ranking is a judgment of frequency and cost, unsupported by data on what working writers submit.

**Low confidence or thin evidence.** Effectiveness evidence for most approaches is interpretive (constitutive, Young, enactment, jeremiad, prophetic, genealogy, Indigenous functions, call-and-response, presence and loci). Empirical narrative effects are small, mostly from health and consumer experiments, and the meta-analyses conflict on print (Braddock & Dillard against Shen, Sheer & Li); no study tests narrative persuasion in op-eds or legislative testimony. Satire's persuasion case is limited. Invitational rhetoric has no evidence for written one-way texts. No validated text-only coding scheme for constitutive address exists, so the recognition signals are untested inferences, and no study shows that any of the proposed editorial interventions improves outcomes.

**Specific gaps carried from the notes.** Black (1970) volume and pages; Jasinski's constitutive work beyond the Douglass chapter; Burke's notion of address; Cheney's tactic triad; Smitherman's 1977 publisher; Fisher (1984), Aristotle *Rhetoric* II.20, and *New Rhetoric* §§78–81 not read in the original; pooled effects for van Laer et al. and Brugman et al., and effect sizes for Zebregs et al.; the 2023 *JMCQ* satire meta-analysis (authors and conclusion not retrieved) and Walter et al. (2018); full texts of Lake, Lyons, Powell, and Yankah; a peer-reviewed source on whaikōrero; Douglass's figure of seventy-two capital crimes in Virginia; whether New York married women's wages were addressed only by the 1860 Earnings Act; Kendall's original "swindle" publication; Campbell's specific reading of the Seneca Falls Declaration; Bishop Hare's role in the 1879 article; replication status of Small et al. (2007); whether Kubin et al. used written stimuli; LaMarre et al.'s sample size (322 or 332). Several canonical works are tiered [S] because only bibliographies or encyclopedia entries were reachable, so any citation the merged report treats as load-bearing should be rechecked at the publisher page.

**Disagreements this synthesis smooths over.** The literatures do not share a concept of "effective" (Fisher's good reasons, the meta-analyses' attitude shifts, Fricker's and Dotson's just uptake, testimonio's collective representation, Charland's formed subject), and seam diagnosis manages these conflicts without resolving them. Grouping constitution, greeting, and identification under AT6 hides their different ethical valences. Adopting the derailment view of strategic maneuvering is a choice that Srinivasan's and Schiappa's arguments contest. Swanson's point about deliberately ambiguous slogans names a propaganda pattern no rule here can catch.
