# Getting unstuck

> Writer's block is not well described as laziness, lack of talent, or lack of seriousness.

Being stuck is usually a problem you can name: too many moving parts, a choice you can't make, a fix you can see but can't yet write, or plain exhaustion. Name the problem first. An exercise helps only when it eases that specific problem. Often the better move is to cut, rest, research, or simply decide.

## What it looks like when it works

- You can say what is stuck before you try to fix it.
- The exercise changes one thing and leaves the rest of the draft alone.
- It is short, low-stakes, and disposable. You are exploring, not writing the final version.
- More than one answer could satisfy it, and every answer is yours.
- It ends with a check: did the problem you named actually change?

## Common ways it goes wrong

- **The "just write" nudge.** Generic advice that could fit any writer on any book.
- **The exercise that writes the scene for you.** "Write the scene where she finally tells her sister the truth" picks the event and the outcome. You get movement, but you lose ownership.
- **Too much feedback.** You can't read your draft without hearing every note you've been given. You need less advice, not more.
- **Choice paralysis.** You can see several good options and can't commit to one. The problem isn't too many variables. It's the choosing.
- **Seeing the fix, hating the execution.** You know what the scene needs, but nothing you write yet is good enough to stand.
- **Exercises when you need sleep.** If you're depleted, more thinking work won't help. Rest will.

## When it's a choice, not a fault

Not every stall needs an exercise. Sometimes the right answer is "you don't need a prompt for this." If the real work is cutting, gathering evidence, or deciding what stage the draft is in, do that. If a revision feels like betraying the book, or yourself, that is protection, not laziness, and it deserves care before technique. Some blocks are about tolerating exposure long enough to work.

## One question for your draft

Finish this sentence before you try anything: "I'm stuck because ___." Is that something a short, throwaway experiment could change, or is it something you need to cut, research, decide, or sleep on?
