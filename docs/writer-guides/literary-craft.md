# Load-bearing prose

> If literary devices can be removed without changing interpretation, they are ornamental, not architectural.

Beautiful sentences are not the goal. They are a tool, and a tool has to carry something. Your style, your images and your form earn their place only when they change what the reader understands, feels or notices. The test is simple: take a device out, or say the passage more plainly. If nothing important goes with it, it was decoration. Polish can hide that, even from you.

## What it looks like when it works

- Each sentence carries a character's way of seeing, not just pleasing words.
- Images come back changed. Each return means something new.
- An unusual form gives the reader an experience a plain telling couldn't.
- Your themes happen in events and choices, not only in what people say.
- The reader works out the insight from what has built up, instead of being told it.
- Your devices still hold at the big moments: the midpoint, the crisis, the ending.

## Common ways it goes wrong

- **Wallpaper prose.** The language is polished, but plainer words would lose nothing.
- **Islands of style.** A few set pieces glow. The scenes between them go flat and functional.
- **The stunt.** The form is fun to describe, but the story would read the same told straight.
- **Furniture motifs.** A symbol keeps showing up and never deepens or turns.
- **Explaining the subtext.** You build something unsaid, then the narrator spells it out.
- **The lecture.** The big insight arrives as a statement, not something the story earned.

## When it's a choice, not a fault

Plain, spare prose can be fully literary if what you leave out is chosen and puts pressure on the reader. Ambiguity works if the reader has enough to hold several readings, not if they're just lost. Cool, ironic distance works if real stakes still come through. Difficulty works if it pays off in sharper seeing. Judge the pattern across the book, not one brilliant or weak paragraph. The fault is a device that does nothing, not a style that is quiet, open or strange.

## One question for your draft

Pick your three most "literary" passages. Rewrite each in plain words. What does the reader lose in the plain version? If you can't name it, what is the original passage for?
