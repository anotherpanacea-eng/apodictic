# Female interiority

> Interiority doesn't evacuate when the scene type shifts.

This isn't about how your female character is looked at. It's about whether a continuous mind sits behind her eyes. Many drafts give a woman a rich inner life in quiet scenes, then drop it once a fight or a meeting starts. The test: in her hardest scene, can you still hear her thinking in her own words? If all you can see is her body moving, her mind has thinned.

## What it looks like when it works

- Her thoughts are hers, specific enough to surprise, not generic "woman thoughts."
- She keeps thinking during action, at work and in group scenes, not just in private.
- She reads other people: she infers, predicts and plans around what they believe.
- Her inner life has friction: between what she wants and what she can do, or what she knows and can say.
- Take everyone else out and she still has things to think about.

## Common ways it goes wrong

- **The voice drops out.** Her phrasing runs through the narration, then vanishes when pressure rises.
- **Body, no mind.** Verbs like suspected, calculated and regretted give way to ran, grabbed and dodged. Check whether the men in matching scenes keep thinking.
- **Labels for feelings.** "She felt afraid" replaces the dread only her history could give her.
- **She stops reading the room.** Sharp in conversation, she reacts only to the surface once danger arrives.
- **The silent reset.** Her inner life switches back on after a gap, with no sign of what she went through meanwhile.
- **Her pain, his growth.** She suffers to change the hero, but we never lived inside her, so her harm reads as plot machinery.

## When it's a choice, not a fault

Thinning can be the point: trauma, survival, masking to stay safe. The question is whether the story knows it's happening. Deliberate thinning shows the cost, such as exhaustion or resentment. Someone, even the prose, notices the gap between her mask and herself. You can see when her mind went quiet and when it came back, and the return carries what happened. The silence makes the reader uneasy. In horror, her fear should fracture in character, not disappear. In historical fiction, limits on what she can do are no reason to limit what she thinks.

## One question for your draft

Take her most public scene and her quietest one. Underline every line of her own thinking. Does the count hold up in the hard scene, and when her mind goes quiet, does the story notice?
