Neri arrived at the tide office carrying a loaf she had no intention of sharing. The bread would explain why she had come before the counter opened. Inside its paper bag she had put a dry sleeve for the pressure strips.

According to the signed inventory, throughout that morning the office held the only key to the tide room. The inventory occupied a frame beside Halden's desk. He had pointed to it whenever the fishers asked to see the readings for themselves.

Halden let her wait in the passage. His own key was visible through the glass lid of his desk drawer, on top of an old medal. He locked the drawer at nine and sat writing letters. Neri could see him through the open office door for the rest of her visit; he did not leave the chair or open the drawer again.

The strip they had been shown at the public meeting ended just before the sluice opened. After that, Halden had said, the recording machine jammed. Neri's father had spent the night pumping out his workshop. He had watched the spindle turn through the tide-room window.

At half past nine Neri took the spare key from her coat. Her father had cut it years before, when he maintained the spindle, and it had been in her pocket since she left home. She unlocked the tide room. Inside, the paper ran smoothly over the drum. The missing hours lay wound beneath the newest readings.

She made a pencil copy against the wall, locked the room, and returned to the counter. Halden accepted the loaf. He did not see the narrow sleeve inside her cuff.

That evening the fishers passed Neri's copy down the quay, weighting its corners with their mugs. Her father looked once at the highest line, then went to fetch the neighbors whose pumps he had borrowed.
