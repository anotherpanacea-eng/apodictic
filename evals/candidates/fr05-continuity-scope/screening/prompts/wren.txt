STORY

For the station's last day, Iona brought two small swallows cast from her mother's mould. One was bronze, heavy enough to stop a door. The other was plaster painted the same dark brown. Her mother had called the design Return, and the word was scratched beneath both bases.

At nine the archivist wrapped the bronze swallow and sealed it in a transparent case in the ticket hall. From then until closing, Iona remained at the counter with the case in full view. The bronze stayed inside, motionless behind its intact seal, throughout the ceremony and until the archivist carried the case away that evening.

People kept calling both birds Return, as if the name guaranteed something. The old porter touched the plaster bird's wing and told Iona where its bronze twin had stood on the mantelpiece. He used to leave his wet gloves there. Her mother used to move them.

Iona had promised the plaster swallow to the primary school. She had meant to make a grand little speech about departures, but the class arrived with drawings of trains that had wings and windows like teeth. Their teacher asked whether they could put the bird by the door, where children who missed home could see it.

During the ceremony, Iona lifted the bronze swallow from the counter and placed it in the teacher's open bag. A pale flake of paint came away on her thumb. The teacher folded the bag's top over twice, slowly enough for Iona to change her mind.

She did not. She asked the children to send a drawing when they had found its place.

The archivist collected the sealed case after the last train. Iona turned off the counter lamp, checked that no one had left a glove behind, and went home with the mould wrapped in her mother's apron.
