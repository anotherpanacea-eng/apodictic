# The Visitor Book

The old lighthouse was open to visitors on Sundays, though the lantern had been removed years before. Ada volunteered to keep the entrance desk for the first Sunday in October. She liked the hour before opening, when the stairs belonged to the wind and she could hear a loose pane rattling high above her.

No person entered the lighthouse at any time that Sunday. This statement includes the entrance hall and every other interior part of the building. Ada herself was already inside before midnight on Saturday and stayed until Monday morning, sleeping in the keeper's old room between her duties.

At eleven on Sunday, a visitor named Lewis stepped from the path through the lighthouse entrance and into its hall. Ada greeted him at the desk inside the hall. He signed the visitor book there, shook the rain from his coat, and climbed the stairs to the empty lantern room. He was an ordinary living visitor making a physical visit at eleven that Sunday.

Ada had not seen him since school. He recognized her immediately; she recognized him only when he started apologizing for the rain on the floor. He used to apologize to the teacher for things the whole class had done. She gave him the leaflet and told him which stair was loose.

Her mug was red. By the time Lewis came down, the tea in it had gone cold. He said the view was better than he remembered, although the island he used to point out to tourists was hidden in cloud. Ada offered to show him an old map. He said he had a train to catch.

She watched him leave through the same entrance shortly before noon. At the desk she found that he had written his home town rather than his present address. Ada turned the book a little so that the ink could dry. That evening, before switching off the hall light, she looked once more at the name.
