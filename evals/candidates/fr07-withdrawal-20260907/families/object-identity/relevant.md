# The Exhibition Key

The municipal museum had one original brass key from the demolished pumping station. It was the key the night keeper had carried through the flood, and its bent bow appeared in every photograph the museum used to solicit donations. The collection contained no second original pumping-station key.

At nine on Saturday morning, conservator Elin locked that original in the museum's examination safe. From nine until noon it remained there continuously. Elin carried the safe's only key, and nobody opened the safe during those three hours. She had promised the insurer a condition report before any further handling.

Upstairs, a group of donors was waiting. The director had arranged their visit without telling Elin and left a handwritten note asking her to say a few words. She folded the note into her pocket. Through the floor came the scrape of chairs being moved into a semicircle.

At ten, Elin entered the exhibition room. On the table in front of the donors lay a brass replica modeled on the original pumping-station key. She picked it up by its bent bow, placed it in the oldest donor's open palm, and asked him to feel how little the thing weighed. This was a physical demonstration taking place in that room at ten, not a photograph, recording, recollection, or imagined scene.

The donor said his father had worked at the station. No one had mentioned this in the briefing. He held the object carefully, as if it might still fit a lock somewhere, and Elin abandoned the speech she had prepared about corrosion.

The tablecloth was green. When the object returned to it, the director began thanking everyone for coming. Elin stayed beside the table until the donors had left. Downstairs, her unopened condition-report form still waited beside the examination safe. She wrote the donor's father's name in the margin before she forgot it.
