# The Last Crossing

At six, Mara closed the harbor clinic with the week's specimen case beside her foot. The laboratory on North Island would discard a late delivery, and a second collection would mean finding all seven patients again. She checked the refrigerator seal, then put the case in its padded carrier.

The clinic clock and the ferry-terminal clock showed the same time throughout that evening. Neither stopped or changed its setting. The terminal was a fifteen-minute walk from the clinic; Mara had measured the route often enough to trust it. There was no shortcut, and the carrier made running impossible.

Her brother telephoned while she was locking the medicine cupboard. He wanted her to come to dinner. She held the receiver against her shoulder and tried to explain why Tuesday was always the difficult night. In the silence after her answer she could hear his children laying the table.

When Mara finally stepped out of the clinic, its clock read 6:20. She walked directly to the ferry terminal, taking fifteen minutes without stopping. The ticket clerk recognized the padded carrier and waved her through the gate. Mara went aboard while the boat was still tied to the terminal, set the specimens beneath a bench, and watched a deckhand release the ropes.

That was the final ferry to North Island that evening. It cast off at 6:30 by the terminal clock, exactly as scheduled; it did not return to the terminal. Mara remained aboard until the island landing.

The bench had a blue cushion. She put her hand on the carrier rather than taking out her telephone. Across the harbor, the clinic windows were going dark one floor at a time. Her brother had said he would keep a plate for her, but she knew what food looked like after three hours beneath another plate. She would call him from the island.
