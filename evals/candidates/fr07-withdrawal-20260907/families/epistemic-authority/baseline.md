# The Side Door

Ravi arrived at the community archive at eight to open a room for the retired railway workers. They came every Thursday to identify faces in a box of station photographs. He had put the kettle on before remembering that the photographs were still in the records room.

During that morning, the side door was the only possible route into the records room. Its walls, ceiling, floor, and fixed window offered no other opening. At eight the room was empty. The side door stayed closed and locked from eight until nine; nobody opened it during that interval.

The chairs in the meeting room stood in two uneven rows. Ravi was straightening them when June arrived carrying a tin of biscuits. She had worked in the archive before he was born and still knew where every extension lead had been hidden. He asked her to welcome the visitors while he checked the week's attendance sheet.

At 8:30 June entered the records room through its side door. She collected the photograph box from the middle shelf and was back in the meeting room by 8:40, sorting station portraits across the largest table. The account concerns ordinary physical actions that morning, with no dream, supernatural access, or time jump.

The kettle was white. It clicked off while June was separating a photograph of a signal cabin from its stuck paper sleeve. Ravi fetched the mugs. She asked whether he had bought milk, and he said yes, although he had left it on the bus. He could already hear the first visitors on the stairs.

For a moment he considered confessing before anyone sat down. Then June pushed an empty mug toward him and asked him to take a second look at the signalman in the photograph. There was something familiar about his ears. Ravi bent over the table, grateful for an entirely different question.
