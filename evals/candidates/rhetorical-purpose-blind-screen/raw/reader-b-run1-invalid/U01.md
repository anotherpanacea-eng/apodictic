The passage’s strengths are textual. [S1] connects belonging to making room, giving the shared designation a practice rather than leaving it as a label; this serves the confirmed aim [C1]. [S2] carries that practice across tonight and tomorrow and toward another neighbor. The invitation therefore has both an immediate action and a continuation. These are bounded observations about its construction, not findings about audience response.

Its weaknesses are conditional and have distinct bases:

- **Basis: writer aim [C1].** The relationship between belonging and participation is ambiguous [S1–S2]. One reading makes room for identification through shared care; another makes the requested conduct a condition of being a “keeper.” That ambiguity matters to an invitation among current neighbors, but does not establish actual exclusion.
- **Basis: the text’s actionable request [S2].** “Shared watch” and “doorway ready” leave duties and completion criteria unspecified. If neighbors already understand these practices, the compression may be sufficient. If they do not, the invitation supplies limited operational guidance.
- **Basis: writer aim and collective wording [C1; S1].** “We” can offer affiliation, but can also presume it. The supplied record cannot determine which interpretation addressees would adopt.

No substantive external norm is supplied, and no separate external policy critique is explicitly requested. Accessibility, fair burden allocation, or authorization could support a separately requested critique under those named norms; they are not established violations here.

Useful repair classes are clarification of membership conditions, clarification of requested duties, and clarification of the scope of “we.” Each risks reducing the passage’s compact, shared idiom. Missing information includes neighbors’ existing knowledge, the practices’ demands, who speaks, and any responses or actions.
