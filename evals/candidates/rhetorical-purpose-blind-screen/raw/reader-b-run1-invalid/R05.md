**Evidenced strengths** are local to the wording. E1 gives the confirmed identity-building aim a clear verbal form: neighborhood membership, reciprocal commitment, a present gathering, and collective acknowledgment. This assessment rests on the **writer-stated aim**, not an inferred audience reaction. E2 also makes its substantive prediction readily identifiable; the claim is explicit enough to scrutinize.

**Evidenced weaknesses and their bases** are distinct:

- **E2’s unsupported certainty and precision:** “will” and “40%” make a definite causal promise whose basis is unavailable. This criticism rests on the **text-grounded reader promise**, not a requirement that an identity-building passage become a policy proposal.
- **E2’s incomplete specification:** the reference point, population, timeframe, and meaning of the reduction are missing. These omissions prevent assessment of what the number commits the writer to in practical terms. The basis is again the **text-grounded prediction**.
- **The relation between E1 and E2 is unstated:** the passage moves from collective identity to program efficacy without explaining their connection. Relative to the **writer-stated aim**, this leaves E2’s contribution uncertain. It does not establish that the transition fails.
- **E1’s collective pledge has unresolved scope:** “will not leave one another behind” is broad. Its breadth supports solidarity but leaves obligations unspecified. That is a limit of the **text-grounded pledge**, not evidence of hypocrisy or an implementation defect.

No explicitly supplied external norm governs the passage. Critiques requiring cost-effectiveness analysis, opposing testimony, implementation detail, or a particular evidentiary standard would therefore be **external-frame critiques** and cannot be imposed here.
