The commitment inventory is:

- **Factual:** Q1 asserts that the speaker personally saw a clerk tear up the speaker’s form on Tuesday; did not hear what the supervisor said; received the stated report from another applicant; and cannot verify it. Q2 asserts the absence of a contemporaneous record. The occurrence of the applicant’s report is asserted; its content is qualified.
- **Causal:** The applicant’s report alleges that the supervisor ordered the destruction. The speaker does not adopt that explanation as verified. No broader institutional cause, motive or pattern is asserted.
- **Quantified:** There are no numerical estimates, rates or prevalence claims. “On Tuesday” locates the event in time without identifying a calendar date. “Everyone waiting beside me” specifies the address’s scope; it is not a claim about everyone’s experience.
- **Representative:** The speaker offers a personal account, not a representative sample. Q3 anticipates differing accounts rather than claiming a shared version of events.
- **Prescriptive and practical:** Q2 asks the panel to recognize the witnessed event while preserving its distinction from hearsay. Q3 offers companionship and proposes remaining together. Neither guarantees support, a favorable decision or agreement among applicants.

**Evidenced strengths:** Q1’s separation of observation, non-observation and report directly serves the writer’s witness-bearing aim and the supplied panel rule. Q2 openly states the documentary limit and gives the panel an explicit instruction against conflating sources. Q3 supports the solidarity aim without requiring identical testimony: “our different accounts” preserves room for disagreement. These are textual features, not evidence of audience acceptance.

**Evidenced weaknesses and limits:** The alleged order introduces a possible explanation that the passage cannot substantiate. The criticism’s basis is the text’s own promise of bounded testimony and the supplied source-distinction rule; it is an inference risk, not a demonstrated rule violation. Q2 manages that risk explicitly.

“Recognize what I witnessed” leaves the requested panel action somewhat open: acknowledgment, evidentiary credit and a support decision are different actions. This criticism rests on the text-grounded promise of a usable panel request. Whether greater specificity is necessary remains uncertain because the decision procedure is missing. Likewise, “Tuesday” lacks a calendar date, but its adequacy depends on context the panel may already possess.

Q3’s “can remain together” could be read as asserting practical feasibility, which the materials do not establish. Its stronger counter-reading is an offer of companionship rather than a logistical guarantee. The possible weakness concerns the scope of that offer, grounded in the solidarity aim; no adverse response is evidenced.

No separate external-frame criticism is supported beyond the expressly supplied panel rule. Requirements for corroboration, formal admissibility, representative testimony or proof of eligibility would be additions.

Useful repair classes are **request clarification**, **temporal clarification if needed**, and **scope clarification for the collective offer**. Their tradeoffs are specificity versus openness, additional detail versus brevity, and practical qualification versus the directness of solidarity. The materials do not establish that all these repairs are necessary. Removing the secondhand report would reduce opportunities for conflation but also remove information whose uncertain status is already disclosed.
