An evidenced strength is the alignment between purpose and scope: the writer offers a concrete episode and expressly limits their authority to speak about others. Another is the economical sequence in C1—waiting, explanation, dismissal—which gives listeners identifiable events to attend to. These strengths are grounded in the writer-stated aim and the text’s bounded promise, without assuming audience effects.

The main possible weakness is the limited development of C2’s promise to explain “what that morning was like” and “why I am afraid to return.” Its basis is the text-grounded reader promise. A counter-reading is that the three-hour wait followed by dismissal supplies the intended explanation through restraint, leaving space for listeners to recognize its significance. The supplied context cannot establish whether that degree of implication is sufficient for this circle.

Elaboration could better fulfill C2’s promise while increasing personal disclosure and reducing the passage’s brevity. Retaining implication preserves restraint but leaves more interpretive work to listeners. Whether surrounding testimony already supplies that connection is missing context.
