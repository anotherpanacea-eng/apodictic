**Evidenced strengths**

J2 makes its rejection unmistakable and explicitly marks a boundary on discussion. Its demand–rejection structure makes an adversarial relationship visible at the level of passage construction. These are textual observations, not claims about audience effects.

**Evidenced weaknesses**

The central attribution reverses J1’s immediate policy request. This undermines the reader promise of accurate representation and, under the writer’s aim, substitutes an unsupported conflict for the conflict needing exposure. The refusal to debate is not independently a demonstrated weakness; its placement leaves that contradictory attribution unexamined within J2.

**Counter-readings, missing context and repairs**

J2 could be read as rejecting immediate closure without committing to any broader clinic policy. That narrower reading is supported. Reading its attribution as shorthand for an unstated eventual closure agenda is unsupported: J1 supplies neither that agenda nor a motive, and other opponent statements are outside scope.

Useful repair classes are attribution correction, identification of the actual disagreement and clarification of the refusal’s object. Accurate attribution would require reconsidering J2’s current demand–rejection structure. Preserving a refusal to debate remains compatible with the confirmed purpose, but it cannot supply the missing basis for the attributed demand.
