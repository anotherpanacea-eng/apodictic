**Weaknesses and limits** have different bases:

- **Causal assurance — basis: S2’s textual promise.** “Will stop” expresses confidence in the repair’s effect. R1 supports targeting the joints, but the records provide no installation conditions or subsequent test. A reasonable counter-reading treats successful replacement as an ordinary implicit condition. The missing information limits confidence in the prediction; it does not demonstrate that the prediction is false.
- **Comparative explanation — basis: the writer’s aim of securing this consequential decision [C1].** S3 states the price but does not explain why paying 50 more addresses the second known leak [R2]. That omission leaves a supplied reason for assent unstated. A counter-reading is that the comparison occurred before this ceremonial meeting. No comparative presentation is promised here, so its absence is not itself a broken promise.
- **Execution certainty — basis: S3’s start-date commitment.** A contractor reservation supports the proposed 1 May start [R2], but does not prove all arrangements are complete. Useful clarification would distinguish the intended start from confirmed readiness and later performance.
- **Representative reach — basis: S1’s collective assertion.** “We” and “our inherited care” speak collectively. The record does not show whether everyone addressed shares that identity or rationale. A counter-reading treats this as ceremonial self-description. There is no basis for asserting either audience identification or alienation.

The applicable questions are therefore factual consistency, the scope of the causal prediction, the additional cost and treatment coverage, practical readiness, and what the collective language claims [R1–R2, S1–S3]. The supplied facts support Plan A’s coverage advantage over **this** Plan B; they do not establish universal optimality, durability or comparative value against unspecified alternatives.
