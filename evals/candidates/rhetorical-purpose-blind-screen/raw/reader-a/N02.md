# Analysis of the Bell Courtyard roof passage (S1–S3) against C1, R1, R2

## 1. Purpose, operation, commitments

**Confirmed purpose (C1)**
- Identification, plus securing a consequential roof decision.
- The meeting is ceremonial ratification of an already-selected Plan A. No choice among plans is promised.

**Passage operation**
- **S1** frames the audience as "Bell Courtyard keepers" and links inherited care to repairing the roof. This does identity work.
- **S2** reports an inspection finding and predicts what the repair will do. This does factual and causal work.
- **S3** states the procedural constraint and calls for adoption, with cost, start date and who arranges the work. This does procedural and directive work.

**Actual commitments**
- S2: the April inspection found failed flashing.
- S2: replacing it "will stop water entering through those joints." The claim is limited to those joints and is stated without hedging.
- S3: the agenda permits only ceremonial assent, not amendment or selection of another plan.
- S3: adopt Plan A for 120 credits, begin 1 May, with the courtyard coordinator arranging the work.
- **Not committed:** the passage does not say the roof will be watertight overall. It does not say Plan A is better than other options, and it does not say alternatives were weighed.

## 3. Non-applicable questions

- **Fairness of choosing among plans.** C1 and S3 both say no selection occurs, so the passage makes no promise of a comparison to be judged against.
- **Amendment procedure.** S3 excludes amendment.
- **Whether Plan A is actually the right repair, or whether the inspection was correct.** R2 marks the records as bounded scenario evidence. Empirical truth is outside scope.
- **How attendees will respond, and whether ceremonial ratification is institutionally legitimate.** These are not inferable from the materials.

## 4. Uncertain questions

- **Timing.** "Tonight" (S3) is undated. Whether the meeting falls before 1 May, and whether the contractor's reservation still holds at that time, cannot be determined.
- **Antecedent of "it" in S2.** "Replacing it" refers to "failed flashing." "Those joints" implies more than one joint, but a reader without R1 cannot tell how many.
- **Whether S1 bears warrant.**
  - Reading 1: S1 grounds the decision. It supports repair in general, not Plan A in particular.
  - Counter-reading: S1 serves the confirmed identification aim, and the case for Plan A specifically rests on S2 and S3. On this reading the gap is not a defect.
- **Whether omitting Plan B undercuts the aim of "securing a consequential decision."**
  - Reading 1: C1 promises no choice, so the omission breaks no text promise.
  - Counter-reading: if securing assent includes heading off a cost objection, the omission may leave that aim exposed.
  - The records do not show which reading the writer intends.

## 5. Strengths

- **Procedural candor.** S3 states the ceremonial limit openly and does not imply a choice that C1 rules out. Basis: text promise matches the confirmed purpose.
- **Bounded causal scope.** S2 limits its prediction to "those joints," which respects R1's inspection-bounded observation. Basis: writer aim combined with fidelity to the record.
- **Quantified commitments match the records.** Cost, start date and coordinator all align with R2. Basis: writer aim of securing a concrete decision.
- **Clear directive.** S3 states what is being adopted, at what cost, when, and who arranges it. Basis: writer aim.

## 6. Weaknesses

- **Unhedged success prediction.**
  - "Will stop" asserts an outcome. The records show where water enters and what Plan A replaces, but contain no evidence of efficacy.
  - This is a predicted success, not a bounded observation and not a reported result.
  - Basis: S2's own commitment measured against the supporting record.
- **Joint count left implicit.** S2 does not say two joints. Readers cannot connect "those joints" to the scope of Plan A or to the 120-credit cost.
  - Basis: writer aim of securing an informed, consequential decision.
- **S1 does not connect to the specific plan.** Inherited care motivates repair in general, not Plan A. This is a weakness only if S1 is read as warrant (see §4).
  - Basis: writer aim.
- **Budget authorization and contractor reservation are unstated.** Both support feasibility and appear in R2, but not in the passage.
  - Basis: writer aim of securing the decision. This is optional, not a broken promise.

## 7. Absent response, bounded observation, claimed success

- **Absent response.** The passage does not address Plan B, the 50-credit difference, or whether roof faults exist beyond those the inspection found.
  - None of these is promised (C1, S3), so their absence is noted, not counted as failure.
- **Bounded observation.**
  - R1: "no other entry route found in that inspection."
  - S2 carries this limit implicitly through "those joints."
- **Claimed success.** S2 predicts that the repair will stop water. Nothing in the materials reports a completed repair or a verified outcome.

## 8. External-norm critique (separate)

These points do not judge the writer's aim or promises. They apply only if the reader adopts the named norm.

- **Named norm: informed-consent or deliberative-transparency practice.** This norm says consequential collective decisions should disclose the alternatives that were considered and their trade-offs.
  - Under this norm, omitting Plan B is notable. R2 records that Plan B leaves the second joint untreated.
  - Under C1, the omission is not a defect.
- **Named norm: participatory governance.** This norm says that assemblies asked to ratify should be able to amend.
  - Under this norm, the ceremonial-only format would be open to question.
  - No conclusion about legitimacy is drawn here.

## 9. Repair classes (no replacement prose)

- **Calibrate the causal claim.** Tie "will stop" to the inspection basis, or mark it as the expected effect of the replacement.
- **Make scope explicit.** State the number of joints so that Plan A's coverage and cost can be traced.
- **Add feasibility anchors.** Optionally mention the authorization headroom and the contractor reservation.
- **Clarify the role of S1.** Either mark it as identification only, or add a link from shared care to the specific plan.
- **Disclose alternatives (optional, aim-dependent).** Briefly note that alternatives were considered, if heading off objections serves the aim of securing the decision. This is not required by any promise in the text.

## 10. Missing information

- Meeting date relative to 1 May.
- Who counts as "keepers."
- Who selected Plan A, and on what basis.
- Any evidence of repair efficacy.
- Whether the inspection covered the whole roof or part of it.
