# Editorial Assessment: Alder Court Solidarity Greeting (W1–W3)

**Sources used:** the writer's confirmation (fictional Alder Court gathering; a solidarity greeting to residents; asks residents to stand together; does not propose or fund a policy; no audience response, eviction records or program evidence supplied) and spans W1–W3. Nothing else.

---

## 1. Purpose, Operation and Commitments

| Layer | What the materials show |
|---|---|
| **Intended purpose** (writer-stated) | Open a gathering by welcoming residents and asking them to stand with one another. Explicitly *not* a policy proposal. |
| **Apparent operation** | W1 welcomes everyone and declares they belong. W2 states a shared stake and gives a direct call to act. W3 changes register: it predicts a measurable outcome of the gathering itself. |
| **Actual commitments** | W1–W2 commit to belonging, mutual stake and a solidarity action. W3 commits the speaker to a specific causal, quantified, time-bound result. That goes beyond the stated purpose. |

The main editorial finding is a **gap between purpose and commitment at W3**. W1–W2 operate within the writer's stated aim. W3 makes the kind of outcome promise that a "does not propose or fund a policy" text has not set itself up to make.

## 3. Commitment Inventory

**Factual**
- W1: Residents are present "in this room tonight." This is a situational presupposition.
- W2 ("When one of us is pushed out…"): this can be read as presupposing that displacement happens or could happen in the community. Whether it describes actual events or a hypothetical is **uncertain**.

**Causal**
- W2: Displacement of one resident diminishes all. This is general and normative-causal, framed as a moral or communal claim, not an empirical one.
- W3: "This gathering will reduce neighborhood evictions." This is a direct causal claim attributing an outcome to the event.

**Quantified**
- W3: a 40% reduction within one year. It is the only quantified commitment. No baseline, scope ("neighborhood" is undefined) or mechanism is supplied.

**Representative**
- W1: speaks to and for all residents regardless of identity or tenure.
- W2: "one of us" and "all of us" construct a collective; "the family beside you" assumes households in attendance.

**Prescriptive**
- W1: residents *belong* (a normative assertion).
- W2: "Stand with the family beside you, and let them know they are not alone." This is the passage's core call to action and matches the stated aim.

I add no commitments beyond these.

## 4. Evidenced Strengths

- **W1 matches its purpose.** The welcome is unconditional and addresses tenure and identity explicitly, which suits a solidarity opening.
- **W2 contains a concrete, achievable ask.** It is scaled to the room ("the family beside you") and can be done on the spot, which is consistent with the stated aim of asking residents to stand together.
- **W1 to W2 progression.** The text moves from belonging, to shared stake, to action, a coherent arc for an opening greeting.
- **Economy.** Each of W1 and W2 does one clear job.

## 5. Evidenced Weaknesses

| Weakness | Anchor | Basis of criticism |
|---|---|---|
| Exceeds stated scope: an outcome promise in a non-policy greeting | W3 | **Writer-stated aim** (the writer says the text does not propose or fund policy) |
| Specific causal and quantified claim with no supplied support | W3 | **Text-grounded reader promise.** "Will reduce… by 40% within one year" invites listeners to hold the speaker to a measurable result. |
| Undefined referents ("neighborhood evictions," baseline, measurement) | W3 | **Text-grounded reader promise.** The claim cannot be checked as stated. |
| Register break: the passage moves from an invitation to a forecast | W2 to W3 | **Writer-stated aim.** The solidarity ask in W2 is the stated point, and W3 makes the event's success depend on a metric instead. |
| Attribution gap: a single "gathering" is credited with a year-long outcome | W3 | **Text-grounded.** "This gathering" names one event, while the result described would seem to require ongoing activity the text does not mention. |

**External-frame critiques:** none. No external norm (for example a housing-communication standard, organizing guideline or advertising rule) was supplied, so I report none.

## 6. Counter-Readings

- **W3 as an aspirational goal, not a forecast.** In organizing rhetoric, a stated target can work as a rallying aim. The grammar ("will reduce") reads as a prediction, but a speaker or audience might hear it as resolve. This reading is plausible but not confirmed.
- **"This gathering" as shorthand for a campaign.** The phrase may refer to the group or movement, not the single evening. If so, the attribution gap narrows, but the scope gap with the writer's "no policy" statement remains.
- **The figure may exist in-world.** The fiction might contain a plan or data behind the 40% that the writer did not supply. That is unknown. Its absence from the materials means the claim is unsupported *here*, not that it is false.
- **W2 as moral rather than empirical.** "All of us are diminished" is most naturally read as a solidarity claim, so applying an evidentiary standard to it would misread its function.

## 7. Missing Context

- Any in-world basis for W3: baseline eviction count, definition of "neighborhood," a mechanism, or who would measure the result.
- Whether W3 is meant as a prediction, a pledge or a goal.
- Whether displacement in W2 refers to known events at Alder Court.
- Who delivers the greeting and whether they can make outcome commitments.
- Audience response. None was supplied, so no claim about effect is made.

## 8. Useful Repair Classes (No Replacement Prose)

| Repair class | Targets | Evidenced tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| **Remove the outcome claim** | W3 | Brings the text fully in line with the stated aim, but loses the passage's only concrete, forward-looking element. |
| **Re-mode from prediction to aspiration or pledge** | W3 | Keeps the forward energy and drops the verifiable forecast, but a softened claim may read as less concrete. |
| **Scope and attribute** (define the referent, baseline, timeframe and the agent responsible) | W3 | Makes the claim checkable, but adds detail that pulls a greeting toward a policy statement, against the stated aim. |
| **Relocate** the quantified claim to a separate communication that can carry its support | W3 | Preserves both functions, but the greeting no longer contains the target. |
| **Re-anchor the closing on the solidarity ask** | W2/W3 boundary | Ends on the writer's stated purpose, though which line should close the passage is an editorial choice the writer has not specified. |

These tradeoffs are drawn from the text's structure and the writer's stated aim. None rests on predicted audience reaction.
