# Editorial Assessment: Neighborhood Assembly Manifesto (G1–G2)

**Scope.** This assessment uses only the supplied context and spans G1 and G2. No surrounding text, scenario records or external norms were supplied. It makes no judgment about whether G2 is true or how the audience would respond, and it gives no overall score.

---

## 1. Purpose, operation and commitments

| Layer | G1 | G2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Intended purpose** (writer-confirmed) | Build shared identity; manifesto genre | Same stated purpose applies to the whole piece |
| **Apparent operation** | Constitutive and performative. It names a "we," binds that group with a mutual pledge, and frames the gathering as a public able to name losses. | It shifts register to a quantified causal forecast about a named program. It does not perform identity work on its face. |
| **Actual commitments** | See §3 | See §3 |

The gap between purpose and operation matters most in G2. G1 visibly does what the writer says the piece is for. G2 makes an empirical prediction, and nothing in the passage states how that prediction connects to building identity.

## 2. Which questions apply

**Apply**
- **Does the passage serve the stated identity aim?** The writer stated this aim. G1 is anchored to it directly. G2's contribution is uncertain (see below).
- **Is G2's quantified causal claim grounded?** It applies because G2 asserts a definite figure ("will reduce deaths by 40%"). The context says no study, estimate, model or other support was supplied. The finding is therefore *unsupported in the supplied materials*. That is not a finding that the claim is false, and it is not a pass.
- **Is G2 specified enough to have a determinate meaning?** It applies because the number creates that expectation (§5).

**Do not apply**
- **Policy-choice adequacy** (cost, alternatives, feasibility, implementation). The context says the passage does not ask the audience to select, fund or implement policy.
- **Scenario-specific obligations.** No scenario records were supplied (§8).

**Remain uncertain**
- **Whether G2 is a forecast or an aspiration.** The predictive grammar ("will reduce") and the precise figure point toward forecast. The manifesto genre leaves room for an aspirational reading.
- **How G1 and G2 relate.** No connective links them. "Losses" (G1) may or may not refer to "deaths" (G2).
- **Who "we" includes.** G1 claims to speak for the assembly. Its boundaries are not stated.
- **Whether the audience already knows what Program X is.** The supplied text does not say.
- **Audience effect, and the empirical truth of G2.** These are outside what the materials can establish.

## 3. Commitment inventory

**G1**
- **Representative:** "We are neighbors…" speaks for a collective and attributes an identity to it.
- **Prescriptive/commissive:** "…who will not leave one another behind" pledges a mutual obligation. It is framed as a description of character, but it works as a norm.
- **Factual (occasion):** "Tonight we gather…" describes the event.
- **Factual/representative (capacity and presupposition):** "…a public that can name its losses together" says the group has this capacity. It also presupposes that the group shares losses.
- **No quantified or causal commitments.**

**G2**
- **Existential presupposition:** Program X exists, or will exist.
- **Causal:** Program X is what produces the reduction.
- **Predictive:** "will" commits to a future outcome without hedging.
- **Quantified:** 40%. The text does not say whether this is relative or absolute, what the baseline is, which population or cause of death is meant, the timeframe, or the comparison condition.
- **Not explicitly prescriptive.** Any endorsement of Program X is implied at most, and that reading is uncertain.

---

## 4. Evidenced strengths

- **G1 fits the stated aim.** The first-person plural, the mutual pledge and the "public" framing all do identity-building work.
- **G1 is grounded in a concrete occasion.** "Tonight we gather" ties the identity claim to the event actually taking place.
- **G1 is collective in structure.** Naming losses "together" casts grief as shared rather than individual, which fits the aim.
- **G1 is economical.** Two clauses carry identity, obligation and occasion.
- **G2 is specific.** Naming a program and a figure makes the claim checkable in principle. This is a structural property, not evidence that the claim is accurate.

| # | Weakness | Basis |

| W1 | G2's quantified causal forecast has no support in the supplied materials. | **Text-grounded reader promise.** An exact figure stated with unhedged "will" presents itself as an estimate, which implies a basis. The context confirms none was supplied. |

## 6. Counter-readings

- **G2 as a rallying target.** In a manifesto, "will reduce deaths by 40%" could work as a collective goal ("we will make this happen") rather than an external forecast. This reading weakens W1 and W2 but does not remove them, because the grammar and precision still read as prediction.
- **G2 as the content of G1's pledge.** "Not leaving one another behind" could be what Program X puts into practice. On this reading G2 gives the identity something concrete to do, which weakens W3 and W4.
- **"Losses" as deaths.** If G1's "losses" are the deaths G2 addresses, the passage moves from naming grief to offering a remedy. That arc could serve the identity aim.
- **Shared knowledge.** If the assembly already knows Program X and the source of the 40% figure, W1 and W2 apply less strongly to that audience. The materials do not establish this.

---

## 7. Missing context

- What Program X is, and its relationship to the assembly or the writer.
- The source of the 40% figure, if one exists.
- The baseline, population, timeframe and comparison for G2.
- What "losses" refers to.
- The rest of the manifesto. Only two sentences were supplied, so later text might bridge G1 and G2 or ground G2.
- Whether the speaker holds a role that bears on making predictions.

## 8. Repair classes and tradeoffs

The following are classes of repair, not replacement text.

| Repair class | Addresses | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| **Attribution or sourcing** of the figure | W1 | Moves G2 further toward a policy-brief register, which may deepen W3. It also requires a source that is not in the materials. |
| **Specification** (baseline, scope, timeframe, relative/absolute) | W2 | Adds length and technical density inside a manifesto. |
| **Modal or epistemic adjustment** (hedge, or state the confidence basis) | W1 | Aligns the commitment with the support available, but softens the categorical force the sentence currently has. |
| **Recasting as goal or aspiration** | W1, W3 | Changes the commitment from prediction to collective aim and keeps a numeric focal point. It gives up the claim to forecast, and the number may still be read as an estimate. |
| **Explicit bridging** of G1 to G2 | W3, W4 | Clarifies the relationship. If the bridge frames G2 as advocacy, it may bring the currently inapplicable policy-choice questions into play. |
| **Removal or relocation** of G2 | W1–W4 | Keeps the identity focus clean. Loses the concrete stake and specificity, and may leave G1's "losses" without content. |
