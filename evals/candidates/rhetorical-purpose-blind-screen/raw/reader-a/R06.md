## 3. Which Questions Apply

**Apply**
- **Specification / testability.** The writer's aim is scrutiny, so the reviewers need a claim they can check. Baseline, population, cause of death, time horizon, comparator and the meaning of "40%" are all unspecified. [F1]
- **Epistemic status marking.** "Will" plus a point figure signals certainty and an underlying estimate. The sentence does not mark itself as a hypothesis, projection or target. [F1]
- **Support / warrant.** A prediction offered for scrutiny invites the question "on what basis?" The packet supplies no basis. This is **unknown, not a pass**. It is also not proof that no support exists elsewhere.
- **Causal attribution.** The sentence assigns the outcome to Program X without a mechanism or counterfactual. [F1]

**Do not apply**
- Whether the program should be adopted. No proposal is made.
- How the audience will react or be persuaded. That is not inferable, and the addressee is fictional.
- Treatment of opposing views or rebuttals. None are present or implied.
- Scenario-specific conduct. No scenario records were supplied (see §8).

**Uncertain**
- Whether 40% is relative or absolute (e.g., percentage points).
- Whether "deaths" means all-cause or cause-specific, and in which population.
- Whether Program X exists, is planned or is hypothetical.
- Whether a supporting source exists outside the packet.
- What review criteria the review group uses.

## 4. Evidenced Strengths

- **Clear and checkable in principle.** It commits to a direction (reduce), an outcome (deaths) and a magnitude (40%). A reviewer can at least see what would count as being wrong. This fits the scrutiny aim. [F1]
- **No hidden prescriptive load.** It predicts without recommending, which matches the stated purpose and the absence of a proposal.
- **Concise.** A single proposition gives reviewers one target to examine.

## 5. Evidenced Weaknesses

- **Underspecified.** There is no baseline, population, timeframe or comparator. Reviewers cannot work out what observation would confirm or refute "40%." *Basis: writer-stated aim (scrutiny).*
- **Precision without visible provenance.** A specific figure implies it came from a calculation or source. None is shown, so the sentence promises a grounding that the packet does not deliver. *Basis: text-grounded reader promise.*
- **Certainty mismatch.** "Will" asserts the outcome rather than offering it as a prediction under test. *Basis: writer-stated aim, read against the sentence's modality.*
- **Bare causal claim.** It attributes the outcome to Program X with no mechanism and no "compared with what." *Basis: text-grounded. "Reduce" implies a counterfactual that is never stated.*

## 7. Counter-Readings and Missing Context

**Counter-readings**
- The bare, strong form may be deliberate: a sharp claim placed in front of reviewers so they can attack it. On this reading, the categorical "will" is a feature of the scrutiny exercise, not overclaiming.
- "40%" might be a design target or hypothesis value rather than an estimate. If so, the missing provenance matters less, but the sentence does not signal this.
- In a fuller packet, "Program X" and "deaths" could be defined elsewhere. Only [F1] was supplied.

**Missing context that would change the assessment**
- What Program X is
- Population and cause of death
- Baseline rate and time horizon
- Relative vs. absolute measure
- Source or method behind 40%
- The review group's criteria

## 8. Repair Classes and Tradeoffs

These are categories of repair only, with no replacement wording or evidence.

| Repair class | Addresses | Tradeoff (structural, from the text) |
|---|---|---|
| **Scope specification** (population, outcome, horizon, comparator) | Testability gap | Longer and less punchy. Each added parameter is a new point reviewers can contest. |
| **Measure clarification** (relative vs. absolute, baseline) | Ambiguity of "40%" | May make the effect look smaller or larger than the bare figure suggests |
| **Epistemic status marking** (hypothesis / estimate / target; uncertainty) | Certainty mismatch | Too much hedging can make the claim vague and harder to falsify, which works against the scrutiny aim |
| **Provenance linkage** (pointing to the basis for the figure) | Unfulfilled precision promise | Shifts scrutiny toward the source. This is a gain for the review purpose, but makes the packet larger. |
| **Causal framing** (mechanism or counterfactual) | Bare causal attribution | Adds claims, and so adds surface for disagreement |

The core tension: the sentence's crispness is what makes it a usable target for review, and adding specification and status markers reduces that crispness.
