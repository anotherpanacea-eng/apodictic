## 3. Commitment Inventory

- P1: Vote next week to close the existing night clinic. (V1)
- P2: Redirect the clinic's *entire* budget to Program X. (V1)
- P3: Begin the transfer on Monday. (V2)
- P4: Cancel *all* clinic appointments *immediately*. (V2)
- P5 (epistemic-prescriptive): Do not compare other ways of providing night care. (V2)

**Factual / presupposed**
- F1: A night clinic currently exists and has a budget. (V1, "existing," "its entire budget")
- F2: Program X exists and can absorb that budget. (V1)
- F3: The council has authority to close the clinic and move the funds. (V1, implied by "Vote")
- F4: Clinic appointments are currently scheduled. (V2)
- F5 (uncertain presupposition): Program X is itself a way of providing night care. This is implied by "*other* ways of providing night care" (V2), but V1 never says so.

**Causal**
- C1: Program X will cause a reduction in deaths. (V1)
- C2 (implicit): Comparison of alternatives would not change the decision. (V2, "no need")

**Quantified**
- Q1: 40% reduction in deaths. No baseline, population, timeframe or comparator is given. (V1)
- Q2: 100% of the budget ("entire"). (V1)
- Q3: 100% of appointments ("all"). (V2)

**Representative**
- None identified.

**Not claimed** (stated to avoid inventing commitments): the passage makes no claim about the clinic's current outcomes, the people holding appointments, costs, Program X's start-up time, or the net effect on deaths of closing the clinic.

## 4. Evidenced Strengths

- **Unambiguous ask.** A reader can state exactly what is requested (P1–P4).
- **Explicit scope.** "Entire" and "all" remove uncertainty about partial measures.
- **Concrete timing.** Dates and urgency markers are given, though see the sequencing issue below.
- **Measurable outcome named.** Q1 identifies a metric (deaths) and a magnitude, so the claim could in principle be tested.
- **Brevity and internal consistency of direction.** Every sentence pushes toward the same decision.

## 6. External-Frame Critiques (Reported Separately)

These rely on norms that were **not explicitly supplied**. They are conditional and do not count as findings against the text.

- **Policy-memo conventions** (if the reader's institution expects them): options analysis, evidence citation, cost, risk, and implementation and transition plans. All are absent. The writer's "memorandum" label makes this frame *relevant* but does not *supply* its standards.
- **Continuity-of-care norms in health services:** notice to patients, referral and bridging arrangements. Under such norms P4 would draw scrutiny.
- **Deliberative or procedural-governance norms:** public decisions that remove services are often expected to show that alternatives were considered. Under such norms P5 would draw scrutiny.
- **Fictionality:** because the council is fictional, real-world harm assessment does not directly apply. The passage's *internal* stakes (deaths, cancelled care) still frame W3 and W6.

## 7. Counter-Readings

- **Cover note reading:** V1–V2 may summarise a fuller analysis held elsewhere. *Unconfirmed;* the context states nothing beyond the passage was supplied.
- **Prior comparison reading:** "No need to compare" might mean comparison was already done. *No evidence either way.*
- **Benign sequencing:** "Monday" may fall after the vote. The text does not settle this.
- **Shared-knowledge reading:** the council may already know the 40% referent. *Not shown.*
- **Deliberate rhetoric:** the curt, urgent register may be a chosen strategy for this audience. Its effectiveness cannot be judged here.

## 9. Repair Classes and Evidenced Tradeoffs

The overall tradeoff visible in the text is that the passage's strengths (clarity, brevity, force) are what most repairs would dilute. The writer would have to weigh that against the interpretability gaps.
