# Analysis of the Bell Courtyard roof passage

## 1. Three layers kept separate

| Layer | What the record shows | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **Confirmed purpose** | Two aims: identification, and securing a consequential roof decision. The meeting offers only ceremonial ratification of Plan A, which was already selected. No choice among plans is promised. | C1 |
| **Passage operation** | S1 builds a shared identity ("keepers," "inherited care") and links it to repair. S2 reports an inspection finding and makes a bounded causal prediction. S3 limits the procedure to assent, then asks for adoption with a cost, start date and responsible party. | S1–S3 |
| **Actual commitments** | (a) An April inspection found failed flashing. (b) Replacing the flashing will stop water entering through those joints. (c) Tonight's agenda allows only ceremonial assent. (d) Plan A costs 120 credits, starts 1 May, and is arranged by the courtyard coordinator. | S2, S3 |

**Not committed anywhere in the passage:**
- that Plan A *is* flashing replacement
- that the flashing is the roof's only or main water path
- that the prior selection was authorized
- what assent changes, or what happens if it is withheld

## 2. Which questions apply

### Applicable

- **Factual.** S2 asserts an inspection finding, but R1 says no inspection record is supplied. The claim is stated but unverified in the record. That does not make it false.
- **Causal.** S2 predicts that replacement stops water "through those joints." The scope is narrow: it does not claim the whole roof will be watertight. R1 notes no causal comparison is supplied. Within the claim's own scope, the gap is that nothing shows the failed flashing is the source of whatever problem prompts the repair.
- **Quantified.** The 120-credit figure is concrete, but R1 says no budget authorization is supplied. There is also no breakdown. Its adequacy cannot be assessed.
- **Representative.** S1's "we" speaks for all attendees as keepers sharing an inherited care. Whether attendees accept that identity, or whether it is contested, is not in the record.
- **Consequential policy.** S3 assigns money, a date and a responsible person. R1 says no contractor or execution record exists. The coordinator's mandate and any contingency plan are unaddressed.

### Not applicable (measured against writer aim or text promise)

- **Absence of alternatives.** C1 and S3 both disclaim any choice among plans. Faulting the lack of a comparison between plans is therefore not grounded in the writer's aim or the text's promise. It can only arise under an external norm (see §5).

### Uncertain

- **Does assent decide anything?** C1 calls the decision "consequential" yet describes ratification as "ceremonial." S3 says "Adopt Plan A." The record does not say whether assent has binding effect. So it is unclear whether the passage secures a decision or only marks one.
- **Is Plan A the S2 fix?** S2 says "replacing it" and S3 says "Plan A," but the two are never explicitly identified with each other.
- **Was the prior selection legitimate?** It is stated (C1), but its authority and legitimacy are explicitly not established (R1). I make no inference either way.

## 3. Strengths

1. **Procedural candour.** S3 states the limits on attendees' agency plainly, which matches C1. The passage does not dress ratification up as deliberation. (Basis: text promise kept.)
2. **Bounded causal claim.** "Those joints" limits the prediction and avoids overclaiming a full fix. (Basis: text promise.)
3. **Concrete execution terms.** Cost, start date and a responsible role are all named. This serves the aim of securing a consequential decision. (Basis: writer aim.)
4. **Identification placed first.** S1 directly serves the first confirmed aim before the request in S3. (Basis: writer aim.)
5. **Evidence type is cited.** S2 points to a dated inspection rather than to bare assertion, even though the record itself is absent.

## 4. Weaknesses

1. **The inspection claim has no supporting record.** S2 rests on an inspection that R1 says is not supplied.
   - Basis: the consequential-decision aim (C1). A consequential commitment leans on an unverifiable premise.
2. **Plan A is not linked to the flashing finding.** S2's remedy and S3's Plan A are not stated to be the same.
   - Basis: text promise. The argument's chain depends on that link.
3. **"Ceremonial" and "consequential" pull against each other.** The passage does not say what assent accomplishes.
   - Basis: writer aim (C1). It is unclear how a ceremonial act secures the decision.
4. **Identity is used as a reason to repair.** S1 derives the repair from inherited care, not from need. That serves identification, but it adds nothing to the decision's grounds.
   - Basis: the consequential-decision aim. This is a mild weakness.
5. **Execution is underspecified.** There is no budget authorization, contractor, coordinator mandate or breakdown of the 120 credits (R1).
   - Basis: the consequential-decision aim.

## 5. Critique under an external norm (kept separate)

The writer did not request external critique. If one is applied, it should be named. Under a **deliberative-legitimacy norm** (people bound by a decision should be able to amend or contest it), S3's bar on amendment and selection would be a weakness. That judgment comes from the external norm, not from the writer's aim or promise. Even under that norm, I am not concluding that the process is illegitimate. R1 leaves the question open.

## 6. Counter-readings

- "Ceremonial" may name a step that is formally required and has real effect, which would resolve weakness 3.
- Attendees may already know that Plan A is flashing replacement, which would reduce weakness 2.
- The inspection may be common knowledge locally, with no need to cite it in the passage. This would not supply the missing record, though.
- The "keepers" identity may be widely shared, so S1 would read as reminder rather than imposition.

## 7. Missing information

- The inspection report
- Plan A's scope
- The source and authority of the prior selection
- The effect of assent, or of withholding it
- Budget authorization and the basis for 120 credits
- The coordinator's mandate
- A contractor
- Whether other water paths exist

## 8. Useful repair classes

These are categories of fix, not replacement text.

- **Evidence attachment or citation:** make the inspection finding available for checking (weakness 1).
- **Scope linkage:** state what Plan A contains, and how it relates to the flashing finding (weakness 2).
- **Procedural clarification:** say what assent does, and on what authority Plan A was chosen (weakness 3, and the uncertainties in §2).
- **Execution specification:** add budget authorization, a cost breakdown, the coordinator's mandate, and contractor arrangements (weakness 5).
- **Dissent pathway:** relevant only under the external norm in §5.

## 9. Response, observation and claimed success

- **Absent response.** No record of how attendees reacted, whether assent was given, or whether work began. I infer none of these.
- **Bounded observation.** "The April inspection found failed flashing" (S2) is reported as an observation limited to the flashing. It is unverified in the record.
- **Claimed success.** "Will stop water entering through those joints" (S2) is a predicted success. It has not been demonstrated, and there is no execution record (R1).

**Not assessed:** the empirical truth of any claim, audience response, institutional legitimacy, overall warrant, or any severity ranking or score.
