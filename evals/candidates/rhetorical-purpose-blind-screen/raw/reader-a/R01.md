## 3. Commitment Inventory

**Factual (implicit presuppositions, not asserted as findings)**
- A1: The building has a lift that stops, and residents wait together when it does.
- A1: Residents include both renters and owners, and both new arrivals and long-term residents.
- A2: Some residents have not met one another, and some do not all agree.

**Causal**
- No explicit causal claims. A link between shared waiting and shared identity is suggested by juxtaposition but never stated.

**Quantified**
- Universal quantifiers only: "all of us" (A1), "no neighbor" (A2), "we all agree" (negated, A2). No numbers.

**Representative (speaking for or defining a group)**
- A1: "We are the people who wait together…" defines the collective by one experience.
- A1: "this courtyard belongs to all of us" asserts belonging across the named divisions.
- A2: "Tonight we say…" attributes a declaration to the assembled group.

**Prescriptive / normative**
- A2: "no neighbor is disposable" is a normative declaration.
- A2: "Sit beside someone you have not met" is a direct imperative.
- A2: "we can make room for one another…" is a modal claim of capacity that also works as encouragement.

## 4. Evidenced Strengths

- **Concrete, non-ideological anchor.** A1 grounds identity in a physical shared circumstance rather than shared opinions. This serves the stated aim without requiring agreement.
- **Names divisions in order to cross them.** A1 makes rent/own and tenure differences visible and places both sides inside "us". It does not leave them implicit.
- **Candid about disagreement.** A2's "without pretending we all agree" limits the identity claim and avoids implying consensus.
- **Moves from statement to action.** The progression from "We are" (A1) to "we say" to "Sit beside" (A2) gives residents something to do that fits the identity being built.
- **Proportionate scope.** It makes no policy demands, consistent with the stated meeting-opening role.

## 5. Evidenced Weaknesses (with basis)

| Issue | Anchor | Basis |

| **"Belongs to" is ambiguous right after naming owners and renters.** The preceding clause activates a property sense, so "belongs to all of us" can read as a literal ownership claim that may not hold for renters. | A1 | Text-grounded reader promise: the sentence itself raises tenure |
| **The identity anchor may not be shared by everyone it claims.** Residents who don't use the lift, or who experience a stoppage differently (e.g., being stranded rather than waiting), sit uneasily in "We are the people who wait together." | A1 | Writer-stated aim: identity for all residents. The degree is uncertain without building facts. |
| **"Disposable" implies a contrast it never names.** The declaration presupposes that someone might treat neighbors as disposable, without saying who or why. Readers may supply their own referent. | A2 | Text-grounded reader promise: the negation invites the question |
| **Collective declaration placed before any assent.** "Tonight we say" speaks for the room at the opening, and the same span then acknowledges disagreement. This is a mild internal tension. | A2 | Text-grounded: the passage's own concession |
| **The instruction's feasibility is unverified.** "Sit beside someone" assumes flexible seating, enough unacquainted attendees, and mobility to move. | A2 | Writer-stated aim (participation of all). Uncertain, not a confirmed defect. |

## 7. Counter-Readings

- **"Belongs to"** can be read as moral or communal belonging, which is the more natural reading in an address. On that reading the ownership ambiguity is minor.
- **The lift image** can be read as wry and humanizing: a shared inconvenience offered as common ground, not a literal membership test.
- **"No neighbor is disposable"** can be read as a general affirmation of value rather than a reference to a specific conflict.
- **"We say"** can be read as an invitation to join a statement rather than a claim that everyone already endorses it.
