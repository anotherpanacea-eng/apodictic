## 3. Commitment inventory (M2 only)

- **Factual:** A group that includes the speaker is presently located at, or positioned in relation to, a specific gate.
- **Representative:** The speaker presents the group's position as shared ("together"). The text gives no indication of how that collective stance was established or consented to.
- **Presupposed:** "This gate" presupposes that the reader can identify the gate, through shared situation or prior text.
- **Causal:** None.
- **Quantified:** None. Plurality is implied without any number.
- **Prescriptive:** None explicit.

I have not added claims about who "we" are, what the gate represents, or why the group is there.

## 4. Strengths (evidenced in M2)

- **Concision.** A single short clause carries a subject, a shared action, and a location.
- **Concrete image.** "Stand" and "gate" are physical and visualizable.
- **Immediacy.** The present tense and the deictic "this" place the reader in the moment.
- **Openness of "we."** The unbounded pronoun invites readers to include themselves. This is a strength under some purposes and a weakness under others; see §5.

### Separately reported: critique under the editor's proposed frame (provisional)

These points hold only if collective identity formation is confirmed as the purpose:

- The sentence asserts membership rather than giving grounds for it.
- "Gate" implies an inside and an outside, so identity may form partly through exclusion.

Neither point is a defect against any confirmed aim.

## 6. Counter-readings

- **Literal report.** People are physically gathered at an actual gate. No identity work is intended.
- **Defensive stance.** Standing at a gate reads as guarding or blocking.
- **Threshold or transition.** The gate is a point of passage, and "together" marks a shared crossing.
- **Ceremonial or ritual.** A formula spoken at an occasion.
- **Contested unanimity.** The speaker may be asserting a togetherness that others would not affirm.

## 7. Missing context
