# Editorial Assessment: K2 Address against K1 Statement

**Scope:** Only K1, K2 and the writer's confirmed aim are used. No other statements, motives or scenario records are assumed.

## 1. Purpose, operation, commitments

| Layer | What the materials show |
|---|---|
| **Intended purpose** (writer-confirmed) | Make a conflict visible without resolving it by consensus. |
| **Apparent operation** (K2) | Paraphrases the opponent's position, declines to rebut it, and frames the gathering as naming the disagreement, not settling it. |
| **Actual commitments** | (a) Attributes a specific position to "our opponents." (b) Commits the speaker to not rebutting "here." (c) Asserts what the gathering is for. (d) Presupposes that a disagreement exists ("our disagreement"). |

## 2. Which questions apply

**Apply**
- **Fidelity of representation.** K2 restates K1, so it should be checked against K1.
- **Alignment with the stated aim.** K2 says it is "naming our disagreement."
- **Avoiding consensus.** K2 explicitly says it is "not settling it."

**Do not apply**
- **Empirical support.** K2 makes no claims about the world beyond the attribution in K2/K1. The clinic's value, costs and outcomes are never asserted.
- **Causal reasoning.** K2 contains no causal claims.
- **Adequacy of rebuttal.** K2 openly declines to rebut ("We will not rebut that position here"), and the writer's aim does not require rebuttal. Faulting K2 for not rebutting would rely on an external norm that nobody supplied.

**Uncertain**
- **"the clinic" vs. K1's "the night clinic."** Whether dropping "night" creates ambiguity depends on context we don't have, such as whether other clinics or services exist.
- **"while we compare" vs. "while alternatives are compared."** K1's "we" could include both sides, the community, or only the opponents. K2's passive voice removes the agent. It is unknown whether the original proposal implied a joint process.
- **"Our opponents" (plural).** The materials contain one statement. Whether several people hold this position is not established.
- **Whether the writer's own position appears elsewhere.** K2 may be an excerpt. Within the supplied span, it does not appear.

## 3. Commitment inventory

- **Factual:** None independent of the attribution. "This gathering is for…" is a claim about the event's purpose, which only the speaker can stipulate.
- **Causal:** None.
- **Quantified:** "this year," carried over from K1. No numbers.
- **Representative:** "Our opponents want the clinic kept open this year while alternatives are compared." Compared with K1:
  - The core action, the time limit and the condition are kept.
  - "night" is dropped.
  - The agent "we" is dropped.
  - K1's imperative ("Keep…") becomes a reported desire ("want"). This is a reasonable rendering.
  - One statement is generalized to plural "opponents."
- **Prescriptive or procedural:** "We will not rebut that position here" is a self-binding commitment. "This gathering is for naming our disagreement, not settling it" prescribes the gathering's function.

## 4. Strengths (evidenced)

- **Core fidelity.** The position, the "this year" limit and the "while alternatives are compared" condition all survive the paraphrase. This avoids a strawman such as "keep it open forever."
- **No imputed motives.** K2 adds no reasons or intentions beyond K1, which respects the stated scope.
- **Restraint matches the aim.** Declining to rebut and declining to settle both fit "visible, not resolved by consensus."
- **Explicit framing.** The audience is told what the gathering is and is not for, which sets expectations clearly.

## 5. Weaknesses (evidenced)

- **One-sided visibility.** *Basis: writer-stated aim.* A conflict has at least two sides. Within K2, only the opponents' position is stated. "Our disagreement" is named but its content is not: the reader never learns what the speaker's side wants. Against the aim of making the conflict visible, the span shows half of it. Whether this is filled in elsewhere is uncertain.
- **Loss of specificity ("night").** *Basis: text-grounded reader promise.* K2 offers itself as a statement of the opponents' position, so it implicitly promises an accurate rendering. Dropping "night" narrows that accuracy. Its practical impact is uncertain, as noted in §2.
- **Agent erasure.** *Basis: same reader promise.* If K1's "we" signaled a shared comparison process, the passive voice hides an element of the opponent's proposal.
- **Unverified plural.** *Basis: same reader promise.* The materials do not support "opponents" over "opponent."

## 6. External-frame critiques

None were supplied. For example, a debate norm requiring rebuttal or a deliberative norm favoring convergence would each produce different criticisms. Neither has been given, so neither is applied.

## 7. Counter-readings

- **The passive voice may be deliberate.** Keeping K1's "we" could imply a shared process, which leans toward consensus. Removing it may serve the aim of not settling. This makes the agent erasure a tradeoff, not simply a flaw.
- **Dropping "night" may be harmless** if the setting makes "the clinic" unambiguous.
- **Not stating one's own position** could be read as courtesy: letting the opponent's view stand without counterweight. The cost is that the conflict becomes less visible.
- **"We will not rebut"** can be read two ways: as respectful restraint, or as a signal that a rebuttal exists and is being held back. The second reading implies an unstated position.

## 8. Missing context

- The writer's own position.
- The number and identity of the opponents.
- Whether other clinics exist.
- Who "we" refers to in K1.
- The nature of the gathering and its audience.
- Whether K2 is complete or an excerpt.

## 9. Repair classes and tradeoffs

- **Restore qualifiers** ("night"). This improves fidelity at negligible cost.
- **Quote instead of paraphrase.** This gives maximum fidelity, including keeping "we." The tradeoff is that it reintroduces the possible joint-process implication and slightly changes the tone.
- **Clarify the agent.** Either keep K1's "we" with its ambiguity flagged, or keep the passive with a note. This trades fidelity against neutrality.
- **State one's own position in parallel.** This directly serves visibility. The risk is that a parallel statement slides into rebuttal, which would contradict K2's own commitment. Keeping the two statements symmetrical and free of argument limits that risk.
- **Correct the number** of opponents (singular, or a scope note) to match the evidence.
