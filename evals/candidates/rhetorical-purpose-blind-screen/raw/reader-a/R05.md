## 3. Commitment inventory

**E1**
- **Representative:** "We are neighbors"; "a public that can name its losses together." These speak for the assembled group and characterize it.
- **Prescriptive / commissive:** "will not leave one another behind" reads as a collective pledge or norm, not a prediction. A predictive reading is possible but less natural.
- **Factual:** "Tonight we gather" asserts that the event is taking place.
- **Presupposition, not an explicit claim:** that the group has "losses." Their nature is unspecified.
- **Causal / quantified:** none.

**E2**
- **Causal:** Program X brings about a reduction in deaths.
- **Quantified:** 40%.
- **Factual / predictive:** stated with categorical "will" and no hedge.
- **Presuppositions:** Program X is identifiable to the audience, and deaths are occurring in some reference population.
- **Unspecified (gaps, not claims):** the baseline, timeframe, population, which deaths, and what 40% is measured against.
- **Prescriptive:** none explicit. Endorsement of Program X may be implied, but that is a reading, not a stated commitment.

## 4. Evidenced strengths

- **E1 matches the confirmed aim.** It uses inclusive "we," a reciprocal pledge, and a shared act ("name its losses together"). The identity is constructed around mutual obligation and shared grief, not opposition to an outgroup.
- **E1 is economical.** Two sentences establish who "we" are, what binds them, and why they are present.
- **E2 is specific.** A named program and a numeric effect make a claim that could in principle be tested, which a vague promise would not.

## 7. Counter-readings and missing context

**Counter-readings**
- E2 may refer to a figure the assembly already knows from earlier proceedings, in which case the gap is in the excerpt rather than the speech.
- E2 may work as a shared aspiration: a hope that binds the group, closer to E1's pledge than to a technical forecast. The categorical "will" and exact number make this reading weaker, but the text does not exclude it.
- "Not leave one another behind" may anticipate Program X, which would make E2 the concrete form of E1's pledge. This connection is possible but not stated.
- "Losses" may refer to something other than deaths, such as displacement or closures. If so, E1 and E2 are more loosely connected than they appear.

**Missing context**
- What Program X is, and the source of the 40% figure.
- The baseline, population, timeframe, and type of deaths.
- What the "losses" in E1 are.
- What surrounds the passage, and the speaker's role or standing.
- Whether any attribution appears elsewhere.
