## 3. Commitment inventory

**Factual and presuppositional**
- B1 presupposes that residents include both renters and owners, some recent arrivals and some long-term.
- B1 makes a possessive claim: "this courtyard belongs to all of us."
- B2 presupposes a meeting tonight, a circle with chairs and a gate with an inside and outside.

**Causal**
- None explicit.
- In B2, "they are not part of our neighborhood" works as an implied justification for the exclusion. It is a stated ground, not a cause-and-effect claim.

**Quantified**
- B1: "all of us," a universal.
- B2: "Only owners," an exclusive restriction, and "Renters," a generic that covers all renters without exception.

**Representative**
- B1: the speaker speaks for "us," explicitly including renters and newcomers.
- B2: the speaker defines "our neighborhood" so that it excludes renters. This contradicts B1's "us."

**Prescriptive**
- B2: owners may sit in the circle.
- B2: renters must stand outside the gate.

## 4. Evidenced strengths

- **B1's paired contrasts** (rent/own, yesterday/years) name the differences that might divide the audience before setting them aside. This directly serves the stated aim.
- **B1's concrete anchor.** "This courtyard" ties the identity claim to a shared physical place.
- **B1's direct address.** The first-person plural "us" fits a meeting opening.
- **B2's operational clarity.** Its instructions are unambiguous. This is a strength only as an instruction. Measured against the aim, the clarity works against the writer (see §5).

## 5. Evidenced weaknesses, with basis

| # | Weakness | Basis |

## 6. Counter-readings

- **Ironic or setup reading.** B2 may be a provocation the speaker plans to repudiate, showing the audience an exclusion so they can reject it. *Limit:* there is no textual marker of irony or quotation, and no later text is supplied.
- **Reported-rule reading.** B2 may relay a rule the speaker did not author, such as one from an owners' body. *Limit:* it is phrased in the speaker's voice ("our circle," "our neighborhood").
- **Procedural-role reading.** Seating may reflect a formal role, such as owners voting on an owner-only matter, not identity. *Limit:* B2's final clause makes an identity claim ("not part of our neighborhood"), which this reading cannot absorb.
- **Figurative "belongs."** B1's "belongs to all of us" is probably affective, not a legal title claim. On this reading, B1 makes no factual commitment about ownership. This also lowers the stakes of B1's own possessive claim, but not the B1/B2 contradiction.

## 8. Repair classes and tradeoffs

These are categories of fix, not replacement text.

1. **Reconcile or remove the contradiction.** Bring B2 into line with B1 and the stated aim.
   - *Tradeoff:* if B2 reflects a real constraint, removing it may leave that constraint unaddressed.
2. **Separate procedural role from identity.** If some owner-only function must exist, scope it narrowly and drop the identity claim.
   - *Tradeoff:* any owner/renter split in a speech about shared identity keeps some tension, even when narrowly scoped.
3. **Mark the voice explicitly.** If B2 is ironic or quoted, signal that in the text.
   - *Tradeoff:* in oral delivery, an unmarked or weakly marked irony risks being taken literally. No reception evidence exists to judge this.
4. **Reorder or reframe.** If an exclusion is being shown in order to reject it, make sure the inclusive frame comes last and governs the passage.
   - *Tradeoff:* this depends on text that is not supplied.
